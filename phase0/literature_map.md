## 0. Verdict on the hypothesis

> **No paper found derives a soliton S-matrix (or a non-relativistic two-body scattering phase) for the Boussinesq equation, "good" or "bad".** The hypothesis survives.

- **Only prior quantisation of a Boussinesq soliton.** Bogdan–Charkina, arXiv:2502.14695 (*Low Temp. Phys.* 51 (2025)), FT. They apply one-body Bohr–Sommerfeld quantisation to the internal (breathing) degree of freedom of the Tajiri–Murakami breather of the **ill-posed ("bad")** equation φ_tt − φ_xx − φ_xxxx + 6φ_xφ_xx = 0 (their eq. 6). The result is a quasiclassical spectrum E(N, P). There is no two-body scattering, and they do not quantise the kink itself beyond its collective coordinate.
- **Experts call the S-matrix open.** Corrigan–Zambon, arXiv:2306.03772 (*J. Phys. A* 56 (2023)), FT, §6: the Boussinesq S-matrix "deserves further study, especially given the existence of classical solutions that do not preserve the total number of solitons".
- **What would change the project's framing (but is not a Boussinesq S-matrix).** Exactly solvable *quantum* lattice and many-body models have known quantum soliton excitations, and their classical small-amplitude long-wave limit is Boussinesq or KdV. The main one is the quantum Toda chain (§4). Its continuum limit gives the *ill-posed* sign of the dispersion (see §1). This is the most concrete existing "quantum Boussinesq solitons" handle I found, and I propose to use it in Phase 2, Route D.

## 1. Conventions used by the main sources

Project convention (fixed, see `README.md`): **u_tt = u_xx − u_xxxx − 3(u²)_xx** ("good"), with u = 2(log τ)_xx.

| Source | Equation as written | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Bonnemain–Doyon 2402.08669 | Hirota form, soliton speed √(1−η²), η∈(0,1); bad case in their App. A | good |
| Corrigan–Zambon 2306.03772 | c⁻²w_tt − w_xx − λ(w²)_xx + κ²w_xxxx = 0 | good |
| Charlier–Lenells 2204.02365, 2302.14593, 2303.10485 | u_tt = u_xx + (u²)_xx + u_xxxx (3×3 RH problem, solitons, breathers) | **bad** |
| Charlier–Lenells 2003.02777 | u_tt + (4/3)(u²)_xx + (1/3)u_xxxx = 0 (no solitons treated) | good |
| Dimakis–Müller-Hoissen–Chen 1805.09711 | matrix "good" Boussinesq | good |
| Rasin–Schiff 1701.00243 | "good" Boussinesq | good |
| Bogdan–Charkina 2502.14695 | φ_tt − φ_xx − φ_xxxx + 6φ_xφ_xx = 0 | **bad** |
| Toda / FPU long-wave limit | ω² ≈ k² − k⁴/12 | **bad** |
| BHK hep-th/0105177; Hikami nlin/0102021 | first-order system (L = ∂³ + u∂ + w, W₃ form); chiral, periodic; no good/bad distinction arises | W₃ |

The good/bad choice is the sign of u_xxxx relative to u_tt. Equivalently it is the choice of real or imaginary time (t → it) in the flow generated by the second W₃ charge of the Lax operator ∂³ + u∂ + w. The chiral CFT literature never makes this choice, because there the "time" is just a label for a commuting charge. (Standard; Phase 1 will verify it explicitly.)

## 2. Classical Boussinesq solitons (input to Phase 1)

- **Integrability and inverse scattering.**
  - Zakharov (1973/74) found the Lax pair. The direct and inverse scattering theory is Deift–Tomei–Trubowitz, *CPAM* 35 (1982) 567, doi:10.1002/cpa.3160350502 (Abs).
  - Charlier–Lenells work with the **bad** equation for everything involving solitons, all FT:
    - 2204.02365: IST, global existence when there are no unstable modes, and solutions that blow up at any prescribed time T;
    - 2302.14593: IST with solitons;
    - 2303.10485: soliton resolution away from transition zones.

    Their "good" paper, 2003.02777 (FT), is solitonless and only gives a conditional representation. (a)
  - In the bad equation, solitons and breathers correspond to zeros of s₁₁ (and s^A₁₁). Real zeros give solitons. Non-real zeros give breathers, which are regular in a region D_reg and **singular for every t** in D_sing. Real zeros can also give singular solitons, depending on the sign of the residue constant. (a: 2302.14593, Lemmas A.3–A.4)
  - *Correction (made during Phase 1).* An earlier version of this map wrongly listed the Charlier–Lenells soliton papers as treating the good equation.
- **Hirota N-soliton.** Hirota, *J. Math. Phys.* 14 (1973) 810, doi:10.1063/1.1666400 (Abs). The explicit two-soliton phase shift for left/right movers is Bonnemain–Doyon eq. (16) (FT). It reduces to the KdV phase shift for small amplitudes. Head-on shifts can have either sign: attractive or repulsive. (a)
- **Resonances and number-changing solutions.**
  - Lambert–Musette–Kesteloot, *Inverse Problems* 3 (1987) 275 (Abs): soliton resonances.
  - Manoranjan–Ortega–Sanz-Serna, *J. Math. Phys.* 29 (1988) 1964 (Abs): soliton/antisoliton interactions (complex phases).
  - Rasin–Schiff, 1701.00243 (FT, abstract and intro): "merging solitons", and solutions that become singular for a finite time.
  - Matrix "good" Boussinesq solitons, 1805.09711 (FT):
    - "pure solitons" do **not** exhaust the soliton content;
    - virtual (composite) solitons appear in head-on collisions;
    - there are inelastic collisions in the vector case;
    - the polarisation map is a Yang–Baxter map for pure solitons, but other classes need to "go beyond Yang–Baxter".
  - Follow-up on "entwining" Yang–Baxter maps: Dimakis–Müller-Hoissen 2001.09688 (FT). (a)

  These are all *classical*. Whether they survive as quantum inelastic channels is open. (b) per Corrigan–Zambon.
- **Bonnemain–Doyon, 2402.08669 (*SciPost Phys.* 18 (2025) 075), FT.** Generalised hydrodynamics (GHD) of the good Boussinesq soliton gas. Their heuristic TBA-like construction needs a choice of "momentum" P(η, ε). They note that Boussinesq, unlike KdV, distinguishes between the candidate choices. Relevant to how semiclassical quantisation fixes the canonical momentum. (b)
- **Defects.** Corrigan–Zambon 2306.03772 (FT): integrable defects. Solitons can *reflect* from a defect, or a defect can decay into solitons. Neither happens in sine-Gordon. (a, by explicit solution)

## 3. Quantum Boussinesq = W₃ CFT integrable structure (no solitons)

All of this is on a *circle* (or a torus) in a highest-weight module. There is no asymptotic particle picture.

- **Kupershmidt–Mathieu**, "Quantum Korteweg–de Vries like equations and perturbed conformal field theories", *Phys. Lett. B* 227 (1989) 245, doi:10.1016/S0370-2693(89)80030-9 (Abs only). Quantum Boussinesq charges at generic c for the first few spins s ≢ 0 mod 3. (a for those spins)
- **Correction.** solv-int/9711013 is by **A. J. Bordner** (*Mod. Phys. Lett. A* 13 (1998) 541), not Kupershmidt–Mathieu. FT. It gives all charges at c = −2 recursively from a reduction of W_∞. (a)
- **Correction.** hep-th/0105177 is **Bazhanov–Hibberd–Khoroshkin** (*Nucl. Phys. B* 622 (2002) 475), FT. It constructs T- and Q-operators (U_q(sl̂₃) structure), with conjectured analyticity and asymptotics. (a) for the constructions, (b) for the conjectures.
- **ODE/IM and its descendants** (all FT):
  - Masoero–Raimondo 1908.11559: third-order opers for all states. (a)
  - Ashok–Parihar–Sengupta–Sudhakar–Tateo 2405.12636 (*JHEP* 07 (2024)) and 2410.11748 (*JHEP* 01 (2025)): eigenvalues, thermal correlators, local currents. (a)/(b)
  - Downing–Karimi–Sengupta–Sudhakar–Watts 2508.16258 (Abs): modular properties of W₃ generalised Gibbs ensembles. (b)

**Relevance.** These fix the quantum *charges*, and hence the conserved quantities any soliton S-matrix must commute with. They give no asymptotic states.

## 4. Discrete and lattice quantum models

- **Hikami, nlin/0102021** (*Nucl. Phys. B* 604 (2001) 580), FT. Its "quantum discrete Boussinesq" is the **Belov–Chaltikian lattice W₃** (hep-th/9303166), a spatially discrete, continuous-time chain. Hikami builds a Baxter Q-operator from affine Toda lattice integrals, with U_q(sl̂₃) symmetry, on a *periodic* chain. No particle or soliton sector is studied. (a)
- **Quantum Toda chain.**
  - Exact solutions: Gutzwiller, *Ann. Phys.* 133 (1981) 304; Sklyanin, *Lect. Notes Phys.* 226 (1985) 196 (citation from memory, not machine-verified).
  - Asymptotic Bethe ansatz: Sutherland, *Rocky Mountain J. Math.* 8 (1978) 413. Mertens, *Z. Phys. B* 55 (1984) 353: particles become solitons and holes become phonons in the classical limit. All Abs.
  - Siddharthan–Shastry, cond-mat/9611221 (*PRB* 55 (1997) 12196), FT: identifies quantum soliton dispersions. (a)/(b)
  - Pustilnik–Matveev, 1507.05639 (*PRB* 92 (2015) 195146), FT: the classical-to-quantum crossover of **KdV** solitons in quantum Toda and Lieb–Liniger. Solitons end up as fermionic particles and holes. (b), with exact results in the two solvable models.
  - **Gap.** Nobody has taken the *bidirectional* (Boussinesq) scaling limit of the quantum Toda soliton S-matrix. (d: this is the opening for Route D.)

## 5. Quantum soliton S-matrices for sl₃-type field theories (templates)

- **Imaginary-coupling a_n^(1) affine Toda soliton S-matrices.**
  - Hollowood, hep-th/9110010 (FT) and hep-th/9203076 (*IJMPA* 8 (1993) 947), FT: the S-matrix is a U_q(sl_n) / Hecke-algebra intertwiner, fixed by matching the WKB limit to the classical time delays.
  - Johnson, hep-th/9611117 (*NPB* 496 (1997) 505), FT: the time delay is "q-deformed" to the exact S-matrix via the regularised quantum dilogarithm.
  - Gandenberger, hep-th/9703158 (FT); Gandenberger–MacKay, hep-th/9506169 (FT): pole structure, breathers, bootstrap.

  All of this is (b): conjectures satisfying unitarity, crossing, Yang–Baxter (YB) and bootstrap checks.
- **Caveat for a₂^(1).** Takács–Watts, hep-th/9810006 (*NPB* 547 (1999) 538), FT abstract: soliton–breather scattering in a₂^(1) is "flawed in both classical and quantum theories". This is a warning for the W₃ / a₂ connection route. (b)
- **Framework.** Faddeev–Korepin, "Quantum theory of solitons", *Phys. Rep.* 42 (1978) 1, doi:10.1016/0370-1573(78)90058-3. FT downloaded from V. Korepin's website. It covers the semiclassical S-matrix from classical time delays, including the non-relativistic case.

## 6. Where the gaps are (input to Phases 1–2)

1. **No two-body S-matrix exists for any Boussinesq soliton species.** The classical ingredients exist: the Hirota phase shift in two kinematic regimes (overtaking and head-on), and the IST action variables via Charlier–Lenells. → Route A is feasible at leading semiclassical order. (d)
2. The **choice of equation** is not cosmetic:
   - the physically realised quantum lattice regularisation (Toda) gives the *bad* sign;
   - the rigorous inverse scattering *with solitons*, including global existence on a restricted class of data (Charlier–Lenells), uses the *bad* sign;
   - the only existing quantisation (Bogdan–Charkina) uses the *bad* sign;
   - the GHD work (Bonnemain–Doyon) and the defect work (Corrigan–Zambon) use the *good* sign.

   This needs your decision before Phase 2 (I flag it, but do not need it for Phase 1).
3. **Number-changing classical solutions** (merging solitons, resonances, virtual solitons) have no counterpart in KdV or sine-Gordon. They put pure elastic factorised scattering in doubt. (b, Corrigan–Zambon) (d, my assessment)
4. **There is no quantum Boussinesq field theory on the line with a particle interpretation.** The W₃ CFT literature is chiral and conformal. The massive, non-chiral quantum theory whose classical limit is Boussinesq has not been constructed. (d, from absence in the searches above)
