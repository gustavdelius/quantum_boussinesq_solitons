What the defect does

On the far side of the defect each soliton component picks up a factor z in the τ-function (CZ eq. 5.5):

z = (sinh θ + s cosh θ ∓ √3) / (sinh θ + s cosh θ ± √3)

  • z > 0: transmitted. The position shift is −(ln z)/k.
  • z = 0 or ∞: the soliton is absorbed by the defect, or emitted by it.
  • z < 0: the field on the far side is singular.

In two-soliton solutions each component transmits independently, with its own z.

Merging solitons

Three exponentials and no constant term (CZ 5.12–5.16). Two solitons fuse into one, or one splits into two, even with no defect present.

At the defect, part of the configuration is reflected and part transmitted. With α > 0 and tanh θ = s/2, nothing passes through: the field beyond the defect is constant.

Scope

This is the good equation only. We showed that this defect family has no real version for the bad equation when the nonlinear term is present. Units: c = λ = κ = 1.

Singular fields (z < 0) are drawn clipped.
