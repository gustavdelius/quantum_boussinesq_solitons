\begin{abstract}
We report an independent mathematical and numerical audit of the two-body Toda Bethe-ansatz package \texttt{phase2/twobody\_toda.tex} (which inputs \texttt{phase2/twobody\_toda\_numbers.tex}) and its four verification scripts \texttt{phase2/scripts/twobody\_toda\_*.py}. Every analytical identity is re-derived symbolically and tested in 70-digit arithmetic using an independent Fej\'er first-rule (Chebyshev-root) Fredholm solver, 45-digit de~Hoog Laplace inversion, and Neville/Pad\'e extrapolators in \texttt{phase2/scripts/audit\_twobody\_toda.py}.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Closed form of $\vartheta_N(r)$ and relation to Phase~2h/2i (Points~7 \&~9):} We \textbf{confirm} \textbf{(c)} that in the iterated edge-then-semiclassical limit ($h\to 0$ at fixed $y_a$, then $Y\to\infty$ at fixed $r=\sqrt{y_2/y_1}\in(0,1)$), the antisymmetrised two-term directional residual equals
  \[
    \vartheta_N^{(1)}(r) = \pi - \frac{4}{\pi}\chi_2(r) + \frac{2}{\pi}\!\left(r - \frac{1}{r}\right), \qquad \chi_2(r)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2m+1}}{(2m+1)^2}.
  \]
  We prove that the Phase~2h/2i residual $R_0(\infty,r) = -\frac{4}{\pi}(\chi_2(r)-r)$ confirmed in \texttt{phase2/audit\_phase2i.tex} is the \emph{forward} (unsymmetrised) two-term limit $R_{12}^{(1)}(r)$. Subtracting the non-vanishing symmetric limit $S^{(1)}(r) = \frac{2}{\pi}(r+r^{-1})-\pi$ dictated by exact two-term reciprocity $H(u,v)+H(v,u)=2B(u)B(v)-2\pi$ accounts for all three structural differences: $+\pi$ arises from the endpoint contact constant $-B(0^+)^2=-\pi$, $-\frac{2}{\pi}r^{-1}$ from the one-loop Jacobian dressing of the slower soliton $y_2=r^2 Y$, and $+\frac{2}{\pi}r$ from $+\frac{4}{\pi}r$ minus the Jacobian dressing $+\frac{2}{\pi}r$ of the faster soliton $y_1=Y$.
  \item \textbf{High-priority audit of the four-term Krein source in \texttt{audit\_frame\_dictionary.tex} (Point~8):} We \textbf{confirm} \textbf{(c)} that \texttt{phase2/audit\_frame\_dictionary.tex} (Eq.~(9), line~431) and \texttt{audit\_frame\_dictionary.py} (line~797) printed an incorrect characteristic source $2f(u)[B(v)-\sqrt{\pi}]$ instead of the true source $(\partial_u+\partial_v)\widehat{H}(u,v)=2f(u)B(v)-2\sqrt{\pi}f(v)$; the error arose from applying $\partial_u$ instead of $\partial_v$ to the one-body edge reference $U(v)=2\sqrt{\pi}[B(v)-\sqrt{\pi}]$. However, we prove by three independent arguments (homogeneous characteristic uniqueness at fixed one-body spectrum, exact vanishing of all $3\times 3$ Schmidt determinants and $2\times 2$ mixed-partial determinants of the true source versus non-zero determinants for $(\partial_{y_1}+\partial_{y_2})A_1(y_1,y_2)$, and spatial cluster separability) that \textbf{the verdict of \texttt{audit\_frame\_dictionary.tex} excluding the antisymmetric two-body freedom $A_\lambda(y_1,y_2)$ on the Toda side survives unchanged}.
  \item \textbf{Head-on directional coefficient and error budgets (Points~2 \&~10):} We \textbf{confirm} \textbf{(c)} that the defined two-term head-on observable $R_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}(y_1,y_2;h)$ vanishes at $O(h^0)$, but \textbf{qualify} \textbf{(d)} its physical interpretation because the algebraically antisymmetric endpoint representative $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{ho}}/h\to (B(u_1)-\sqrt{\pi})(B(u_2)-\sqrt{\pi})$ does not vanish after subtracting $4rY$. We also \textbf{qualify} the error budgets at large $Y$ in the $h$-fit ($\kappa_1=1.453>1$ at $Y=64, h=0.02$) and at small $r=0.25$ in the $1/Y$-fit ($y_2=r^2Y=2$ at $Y=32$, causing a $2684\times$ truncation-error jump in $S^{(1)}(0.25)$ that can be eliminated by using the exact one-body symmetric limit).
\end{enumerate}
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Conventions and Independence from Earlier Refuted Claims (Points 3 \& 5)}

\paragraph{Good Boussinesq and Toda scaling conventions (Point 3).}
The package works in the fixed project convention (\texttt{AGENTS.md}) for the \textbf{good} Boussinesq equation $u_{tt}=u_{xx}-u_{xxxx}-3(u^2)_{xx}$ with $u=\varphi_x$, $L=\frac12\varphi_t^2-\frac12\varphi_x^2+\varphi_x^3-\frac12\varphi_{xx}^2$, and $\Omega=\int\dd\pi\wedge\dd\varphi$. In the semiclassical Toda dictionary (\texttt{phase2/audit\_toda\_dictionary.tex}, \texttt{phase2/audit\_phase2i.tex}), the edge parameter is $b=\ee^{-1/2}$, the rapidities are $\kappa_a=\sqrt{h y_a/b}$, and the rescaled energies and momenta are $E_{\mathrm{good}}(\kappa_a)=\frac{2}{3}b(\sinh 3\kappa_a-3\sinh\kappa_a)$, $P_{\mathrm{good}}(\kappa_a)=4b(\kappa_a\cosh\kappa_a-\sinh\kappa_a)$, and $M_{\mathrm{good}}(\kappa_a)=2b\sinh\kappa_a$ \textbf{(a)}/\textbf{(c)}. The one-loop Toda sound speed $c_s = 1 - \frac{3}{4\pi}h + O(h^2)$ matches our independent 5-point Fej\'er-I Neville extrapolation $(c_s-1)/h \to -0.23873241498$, agreeing with $-\frac{3}{4\pi}=-0.23873241464$ to $3.4\times 10^{-10}$ \textbf{(c)}.

Regarding the defect scale convention (\texttt{AGENTS.md}: scattering uses $s=\sigma_{\mathrm{vac}}$, whereas the spectrum uses $\eta\sigma_{\mathrm{vac}}$; see \texttt{phase2/audit\_smx.tex} and \texttt{phase2/errata.tex} Item~E1), \texttt{phase2/twobody\_toda.tex} operates entirely within the Toda Bethe ansatz and its Wiener--Hopf edge limit and never invokes the Boussinesq integrable defect transmission matrix or the Corrigan--Zambon (\texttt{arXiv:2306.03772}, verified against repository dictionary) defect potential. Its results are therefore unaffected by the defect $s$ convention \textbf{(c)}.

\paragraph{Reliance on earlier audited results (Point 5).}
We cross-checked \texttt{phase2/twobody\_toda.tex} against all prior audit reports (\texttt{audit\_phase2i.tex}, \texttt{audit\_frame\_dictionary.tex}, \texttt{audit\_toda\_dictionary.tex}, \texttt{audit\_onebody\_scheme.tex}, \texttt{audit\_smx.tex}, and \texttt{errata.tex}). \subsection{Exact Origin of the Discrepancy in the Krein Characteristic Source}

In the edge limit $p_a=q+h u_a$ ($u_1=u>u_2=v>0$), the directional Bethe phase $N_{12}/h$ and endpoint reference $U_{12}/h$ converge to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H_and_U}
  H(u,v) = 2\int_0^v f(u-v+t)B(t)\,\dd t, \qquad U(v) = 2c\bigl[B(v)-c\bigr], \qquad c = B(0^+) = \sqrt{\pi},
\end{equation}
where $B(u)=\Phi'(u)$ and $f(u)=B'(u)=\Phi''(u)$ \textbf{(c)}. Differentiating $H(u,v)$ along the characteristic direction $\partial_u+\partial_v$ (along which $u-v$ is constant) gives purely the upper-limit boundary contribution:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:char_H}
  (\partial_u+\partial_v)H(u,v) = \left.\frac{\dd}{\dd\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0} 2\int_0^{v+\epsilon} f(u-v+t)B(t)\,\dd t = 2f(u)B(v) \qquad (u>v>0).
\end{equation}
Crucially, the one-body endpoint subtraction $U(v)=2c[B(v)-c]$ depends \emph{only on the second argument $v$}: $\partial_u U(v)=0$ and $\partial_v U(v)=2cB'(v)=2cf(v)$. Therefore, the edge limit $\widehat{H}(u,v)=H(u,v)-U(v)$ of the four-term subtraction $F_{12}=N_{12}-U_{12}$ satisfies \textbf{(c)}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:true_krein_source}
  (\partial_u+\partial_v)\widehat{H}(u,v) = 2f(u)B(v) - 2cf(v) = 2f(u)B(v) - 2\sqrt{\pi}f(v),
\end{equation}
which is Eq.~(14) of \texttt{phase2/twobody\_toda.tex}.

By contrast, \texttt{phase2/audit\_frame\_dictionary.tex} (Eq.~(9), line~431) and \texttt{phase2/scripts/audit\_frame\_dictionary.py} (line~797) wrote
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wrong_krein_source}
  (\partial_{u_1}+\partial_{u_2})F_{\mathrm{sub}}(u_1,u_2) \stackrel{\text{printed}}{=} 2f(u_1)\bigl[B(u_2)-\sqrt{\pi}\bigr] = 2f(u_1)B(u_2) - 2\sqrt{\pi}f(u_1).
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:wrong_krein_source} committed the error of replacing $\partial_{u_2}B(u_2)=f(u_2)$ with $f(u_1)$ in the subtracted term, as if $2\sqrt{\pi}c$ had been subtracted inside the $t$-integrand of $H(u_1,u_2)$ at the upper limit $t=u_2$ rather than subtracted as a function of $u_2$ alone. The difference between the true source~\eqref{eq:true_krein_source} and the printed formula~\eqref{eq:wrong_krein_source} is
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{true}}(u,v) - S_{\mathrm{printed}}(u,v) = 2\sqrt{\pi}\bigl[f(u)-f(v)\bigr] \neq 0 \qquad (u\neq v).
\end{equation}
In Check~3 of \texttt{audit\_twobody\_toda.py}, we verify Eq.~\eqref{eq:true_krein_source} both symbolically and numerically to 45-digit precision using de~Hoog Laplace inversion and 4th-order central differences along $u+\epsilon, v+\epsilon$: at $(u,v)=(4,1)$, $(\partial_u+\partial_v)\widehat{H}=-0.34710425466292045535$, matching $S_{\mathrm{true}}(4,1)$ to $2.06\times 10^{-21}$ while disagreeing with $S_{\mathrm{printed}}(4,1)=+0.83774806069206012686$ by $1.1848523153549805822$ \textbf{(c)}.

\subsection{Why the Verdict of \texttt{audit\_frame\_dictionary.tex} Excluding \texorpdfstring{$A_\lambda(y_1,y_2)$}{A\_lambda(y1,y2)} Survives Unchanged}

In \texttt{phase2/frame\_dictionary.tex}, a one-parameter family of classical antisymmetric two-body observables
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A_lambda}
  A_\lambda(y_1,y_2) = \lambda\,\frac{(y_1-y_2)(y_1 y_2)^{3/2}}{y_1^2+y_2^2}
\end{equation}
was shown to preserve antisymmetry, scale invariance, and the overtaking classical limit $H_{\mathrm{cl}}(Y,r)$. In \texttt{phase2/audit\_frame\_dictionary.tex}, Theorem~3.2 concluded that cluster separability and the Krein characteristic PDE exclude $A_\lambda(y_1,y_2)$ ($\lambda\neq 0$) on the Toda side. Because \texttt{audit\_frame\_dictionary.tex} cited the rank-1 factorisation of the printed source~\eqref{eq:wrong_krein_source}, we must determine whether Theorem~3.2 survives when $S_{\mathrm{printed}}$ is replaced by the true source $S_{\mathrm{true}}(u,v)=2f(u)B(v)-2cf(v)$. We prove that \textbf{the exclusion verdict survives completely}, for three independent reasons \textbf{(c)}:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Homogeneous characteristic uniqueness at fixed one-body spectrum:}
  The functions $B(u)=\Phi'(u)$ and $f(u)=B'(u)$ are one-body spectral quantities determined solely by the single-hole Wiener--Hopf dressing on the half-line. Any admissible two-body modification $\widetilde{H}(u,v)=\widehat{H}(u,v)+\Delta\widehat{H}(u,v)$ that leaves the one-body dispersion relation $\Phi(u)$ (and hence $B(u)$, $f(u)$, and $S_{\mathrm{true}}(u,v)$) unchanged must satisfy the \emph{same} inhomogeneous Krein PDE~\eqref{eq:true_krein_source}. Consequently, the difference $\Delta\widehat{H}(u,v)$ must satisfy the \textbf{homogeneous} characteristic equation
  \begin{equation}
    (\partial_u+\partial_v)\Delta\widehat{H}(u,v) = 0 \qquad (u>v>0), \qquad \Delta\widehat{H}(u,0)=0,
  \end{equation}
  whose unique solution on $u\ge v\ge 0$ is $\Delta\widehat{H}(u,v)\equiv 0$, \emph{independently of the form of the inhomogeneous source $S_{\mathrm{true}}(u,v)$}. In fact, lines~800--804 of \texttt{audit\_frame\_dictionary.py} tested $A_\lambda(y_1,y_2)$ by differentiating $A_1(y_1,y_2)$ directly without ever evaluating $S_{\mathrm{printed}}$ in code.

  \item \textbf{Exact algebraic PDE invariants ($3\times 3$ Schmidt determinant and $2\times 2$ mixed-partial determinant):}
  Although $S_{\mathrm{true}}(u,v)=2f(u)B(v)-2cf(v)$ has Schmidt rank~2 rather than rank~1 (being a sum of $f(u)\otimes B(v)$ and $1\otimes f(v)$), it satisfies two exact algebraic identities that do not depend on knowing $B$ or $f$ in advance:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Every $3\times 3$ determinant $\det_{1\le i,j\le 3} S_{\mathrm{true}}(u_i,v_j)$ vanishes identically because $S_{\mathrm{true}}$ lies in a 2-dimensional tensor subspace; and
    \item Taking $\partial_u$ annihilates the one-body subtraction $-2cf(v)$, so the mixed partial derivative
    \[
      \partial_u\partial_v(\partial_u+\partial_v)\widehat{H}(u,v) = \partial_u\partial_v S_{\mathrm{true}}(u,v) = 2f'(u)f(v)
    \]
    is \emph{strictly rank-1 separable}, meaning every $2\times 2$ cross-determinant vanishes identically:
    \[
      \det_{1\le i,j\le 2}\bigl[\partial_{u_i}\partial_{v_j} S_{\mathrm{true}}(u_i,v_j)\bigr] \equiv 0.
    \]
  \end{enumerate}
  In Check~3 of \texttt{audit\_twobody\_toda.py}, we evaluate both determinants symbolically for $D_A(y_1,y_2)=(\partial_{y_1}+\partial_{y_2})A_1(y_1,y_2)$: at $(y_1)\in(1,2,3)$ and $(y_2)\in(4,5,6)$, the $3\times 3$ Schmidt determinant is
  \[
    \det_{1\le i,j\le 3} D_A(y_{1,i},y_{2,j}) = -\frac{19469608614112801}{66954376636977070080000}\sqrt{30} \neq 0,
  \]
  and at $(y_1)\in(1,2)$, $(y_2)\in(4,5)$, the $2\times 2$ mixed-partial determinant is
  \[
    \det_{1\le i,j\le 2} \partial_{y_1}\partial_{y_2}D_A(y_{1,i},y_{2,j}) = \frac{8835448337}{86838197512500}\sqrt{5} \neq 0.
  \]
  Hence $A_\lambda(y_1,y_2)$ cannot be absorbed into any rank-2 Krein source of the form $2\tilde{f}(y_1)\tilde{B}(y_2)-2c\tilde{f}(y_2)$ for \emph{any} one-body functions $(\tilde{B},\tilde{f})$.

  \item \textbf{Spatial cluster separability and $r$-analyticity:}
  Independently of the Krein PDE, any canonical frame transformation generated by local one-body asymptotic invariants $\sum_{a\neq b} c_k Q_k(p_a)\tilde{Q}_k(p_b)$ has finite Schmidt rank ($\det_{3\times 3}=0$), whereas $\det_{1\le i,j\le 3} A_1(y_{1,i},y_{2,j}) = -\frac{1054728\sqrt{30}}{291949675}\neq 0$. Moreover, $A_\lambda(Y,r^2Y)=\lambda\frac{r^3(1-r^2)}{1+r^4}Y^2$ has rational poles at $r^4=-1$ ($r=\ee^{\pm\ii\pi/4}$) on the unit circle, whereas the Toda Bethe kernel has logarithmic branch singularities only at $r=\pm 1$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Analytic Derivation from the Edge-Limit Bethe Equations (Point 7)}

In the edge limit $p_a=q+h u_a$ with $u_a=u(y_a)$ fixed by $\Phi(u(y))=\frac{4}{3}y^{3/2}$, the large-$y$ Wiener--Hopf asymptotics of $\Phi(u)$, $B(u)=\Phi'(u)$, and $f(u)=B'(u)$ follow from expanding $\widehat{\Phi}(s)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{s^2}g(s)$ around $s\to 0^+$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1body_asy}
  u(y) = y - A\log y + c_0 + \frac{d_0}{y} + \frac{e_0}{y^2} + O(y^{-3}), \qquad A = \frac{1}{2\pi}, \qquad d_0 = \frac{1}{48} - \frac{1}{4\pi^2}.
\end{equation}
Since $B(u(y))u'(y)=\frac{\dd}{\dd y}\Phi(u(y))=2\sqrt{y}$, differentiating $u(y)$ yields the exact Jacobian expansion \textbf{(c)}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B_jacobian}
  B\bigl(u(y)\bigr) = \frac{2\sqrt{y}}{u'(y)} = 2\sqrt{y}\left[1 + \frac{A}{y} + \frac{A^2+d_0}{y^2} + \frac{A^3+2Ad_0+2e_0}{y^3} + O(y^{-4})\right],
\end{equation}
and differentiating a second time gives $f(u)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{u}}-\frac{A}{u^{3/2}}+O(u^{-5/2})$.

Substituting $u_1=u(Y)$, $u_2=u(r^2Y)$, and $z=Y t$ ($t\in(0,r^2)$) into the regularised integral~\eqref{eq:audit_eq16} gives
\[
  u(Y)-u(r^2Y)+u(Yt) = Y(1-r^2+t) + A\log\!\left(\frac{r^2}{t}\right) - A\log(1-r^2+t) + O(Y^{-1}).
\]
Expanding $f(w)=w^{-1/2}-Aw^{-3/2}+O(w^{-5/2})$ at $w=Y(1-r^2+t)+\Delta$ gives
\[
  f\bigl(u(Y)-u(r^2Y)+u(Yt)\bigr) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{Y(1-r^2+t)}} - \frac{A\bigl[2+\log(r^2/t)\bigr]}{2Y^{3/2}(1-r^2+t)^{3/2}} + O(Y^{-5/2}\log Y).
\]
Integrating the $O(Y^{-1/2})$ term against $4Y^{3/2}\sqrt{t}\,\dd t$ reproduces the classical overtaking benchmark
\[
  H_{\mathrm{cl}}(Y,r) = 4Y\int_0^{r^2}\sqrt{\frac{t}{1-r^2+t}}\,\dd t = 4rY + 2Y(1-r^2)\log\!\left(\frac{1-r}{1+r}\right),
\]
 while the $O(Y^{-3/2})$ term gives the exact $Y\to\infty$ forward directional residual \textbf{(c)}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R12_integral}
  R_{12}^{(1)}(r) = \lim_{Y\to\infty}\bigl[H(u(Y),u(r^2Y))-H_{\mathrm{cl}}(Y,r)\bigr] = -2A\int_0^{r^2}\frac{\sqrt{t}\,\bigl[2+\log(r^2/t)\bigr]}{(1-r^2+t)^{3/2}}\,\dd t.
\end{equation}
To evaluate Eq.~\eqref{eq:R12_integral} in closed form, set $s=\sqrt{t/(1-r^2+t)}\in(0,r)$, so that $t=(1-r^2)s^2/(1-s^2)$, $1-r^2+t=(1-r^2)/(1-s^2)$, and $\frac{\sqrt{t}}{(1-r^2+t)^{3/2}}\,\dd t = \frac{2s^2}{1-s^2}\,\dd s$. Then $\log(r^2/t)-\log(1-r^2+t)=-2\log(s/r)-\log(1-r^2)$, and using $\frac{s^2}{1-s^2}=\frac{\dd}{\dd s}\bigl[-s+\frac12\log\frac{1+s}{1-s}\bigr]$ to integrate by parts (the boundary terms vanish at $s=0$ as $O(s^3\log s)$ and at $s=r$ since $\log(r/r)=0$) yields \textbf{(c)}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R12_closed}
  R_{12}^{(1)}(r) = 8A\int_0^r \log\!\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\frac{s^2}{1-s^2}\,\dd s = -8A\bigl[\chi_2(r)-r\bigr] = -\frac{4}{\pi}\bigl[\chi_2(r)-r\bigr].
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact Comparison with \texorpdfstring{$R_0(\infty,r)$}{R0(infinity,r)} of Phase~2h/2i: Origin of \texorpdfstring{$\pi$}{pi} and \texorpdfstring{$r^{-1}$}{r\^{}(-1)} (Point 9)}

Equation~\eqref{eq:R12_closed} establishes that the forward two-term residual $R_{12}^{(1)}(r)$ is \emph{identically equal} to the Phase~2h/2i constant-term residual confirmed in \texttt{phase2/audit\_phase2i.tex}:
\begin{equation}
  R_{12}^{(1)}(r) \equiv R_0(\infty,r) = -\frac{4}{\pi}\bigl[\chi_2(r)-r\bigr].
\end{equation}
Why, then, does the antisymmetrised residual $\vartheta_N^{(1)}(r)=\frac12(R_{12}^{(1)}(r)-R_{21}^{(1)}(r))$ equal $\pi - \frac{4}{\pi}\chi_2(r) + \frac{2}{\pi}(r-r^{-1})$ rather than $R_0(\infty,r)$?

In Phase~2h/2i, only the forward branch $R_{12}$ ($y_1>y_2$) was computed, under the tacit assumption that the two-term residual was antisymmetric. As proved in \texttt{phase2/audit\_phase2i.tex} (Claim~3) and Section~3.1 of \texttt{twobody\_toda.tex}, integration by parts on $H(u,v)=2\int_0^v f(u-v+t)B(t)\,\dd t$ yields the exact two-term reciprocity failure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reciprocity_H}
  H(u_1,u_2) + H(u_2,u_1) = 2B(u_1)B(u_2) - 2B(0^+)^2 = 2B(u_1)B(u_2) - 2\pi.
\end{equation}
Meanwhile, in the classical benchmark, $\delta_T(y_1,y_2)+\delta_T(y_2,y_1)=0$, whereas the classical frame terms sum to $(C_{12}+C_{21})/h \to 8\sqrt{y_1 y_2}=8rY$. Therefore, the symmetric combination $S(Y,r)=\frac12[R_{12}(Y,r)+R_{21}(Y,r)]$ is controlled purely by the one-body Jacobian $B(u(y))$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:B_jacobian}:
\begin{align}
  S(Y,r) &= B\bigl(u(Y)\bigr)B\bigl(u(r^2Y)\bigr) - \pi - 4rY \nonumber\\
  &= 4rY\left(1 + \frac{A}{Y} + \frac{A^2+d_0}{Y^2} + \cdots\right)\!\left(1 + \frac{A}{r^2Y} + \frac{A^2+d_0}{r^4Y^2} + \cdots\right) - \pi - 4rY \nonumber\\
  &= \underbrace{\left[\frac{2}{\pi}\!\left(r + \frac{1}{r}\right) - \pi\right]}_{S^{(1)}(r)} + \left[\frac{1}{\pi^2 r} + 4\!\left(\frac{1}{4\pi^2}+d_0\right)\!\left(r + \frac{1}{r^3}\right)\right]\frac{1}{Y} + O(r^{-5}Y^{-2}). \label{eq:S_expansion}
\end{align}
Since $\vartheta_N(Y,r)=\frac12(R_{12}-R_{21}) = R_{12}(Y,r) - S(Y,r)$, subtracting $S^{(1)}(r)$ from $R_{12}^{(1)}(r)=R_0(\infty,r)$ gives \textbf{(c)}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vartheta_decomp}
  \vartheta_N^{(1)}(r) = R_0(\infty,r) - S^{(1)}(r) = \left[-\frac{4}{\pi}\chi_2(r) + \frac{4}{\pi}r\right] - \left[\frac{2}{\pi}r + \frac{2}{\pi}\frac{1}{r} - \pi\right] = \pi - \frac{4}{\pi}\chi_2(r) + \frac{2}{\pi}\!\left(r - \frac{1}{r}\right).
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:vartheta_decomp} accounts for every structural difference between $\vartheta_N^{(1)}(r)$ and $R_0(\infty,r)$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{The $+\pi$ constant:} Arises from $-\bigl(-B(0^+)^2\bigr)=+\pi$ in the two-term reciprocity identity~\eqref{eq:reciprocity_H}, i.e., the non-vanishing boundary density $B(0^+)=\sqrt{\pi}$ at the spectral edge.
  \item \textbf{The $-\frac{2}{\pi}r^{-1}$ pole as $r\to 0^+$:} Arises from the one-loop Jacobian dressing $B(u(y_2))=2\sqrt{y_2}\bigl[1+\frac{1}{2\pi y_2}+\cdots\bigr]$ of the \emph{slower} soliton $y_2=r^2Y$ multiplying the classical factor $2\sqrt{y_1}=2\sqrt{Y}$ in $B(u(y_1))B(u(y_2))-4rY$.
  \item \textbf{The reduction of $+\frac{4}{\pi}r$ to $+\frac{2}{\pi}r$:} Half of the $+\frac{4}{\pi}r$ linear term in $R_0(\infty,r)$ is cancelled by the one-loop Jacobian dressing $+\frac{2}{\pi}r$ of the \emph{faster} soliton $y_1=Y$ multiplying $2\sqrt{y_2}=2r\sqrt{Y}$ in $S^{(1)}(r)$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Audit of the Error Budgets (Point 2)}
\label{sec:budgets}
% ==============================================================================

The package separates three error sources: quadrature mesh error, $h\to 0$ fit spread across alternative polynomial bases, and $Y\to\infty$ extrapolation uncertainty. Our audit confirms that the separation is structurally transparent, but identifies three quantitative weaknesses that require \textbf{qualification}:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Breakdown of the small-$\kappa$ regime at $Y=64$ in the $h\to 0$ fit:}
  The package's primary $h\to 0$ fit (\texttt{quartic7}) uses the 7 smallest values $h\in\{0.001,0.0015,0.002,0.003,0.005,0.01,0.02\}$, while its comparison family includes \texttt{quintic8} ($h\le 0.03$), \texttt{quad4} ($h\le 0.003$), and \texttt{hlog7} ($h\le 0.01$ with $h^2\log h, h^3\log h$). However, the Boussinesq rapidity is $\kappa_1=\sqrt{hY/b}$ with $b=\ee^{-1/2}\approx 0.60653$. At $Y=64$:
  \[
    \kappa_1(Y=64, h=0.02) = 1.4526 > 1, \qquad \kappa_1(Y=64, h=0.03) = 1.7791 > 1.
  \]
  Because $\cosh\kappa_1$ and $\sinh\kappa_1$ are no longer in the small-$hY$ Taylor regime at $h\in\{0.02,0.03\}$ when $Y=64$, the $h$-fit spread on $R_{12}$ explodes by $3800\times$ between $Y=4$ ($3.11\times 10^{-8}$) and $Y=64$ ($1.19\times 10^{-4}$), and the discrepancy between the finite-sea extrapolation and the exact Wiener--Hopf value grows from $3.26\times 10^{-9}$ at $Y=4$ to $4.33\times 10^{-5}$ at $Y=64$. Thus, for large $Y$, $h$ must be scaled so that $hY\ll b$ rather than held on an $Y$-independent $h$-grid.

  \item \textbf{$37.23\times$ inflation of \texttt{mesh\_error} by the ill-conditioned \texttt{hlog7} basis:}
  In \texttt{twobody\_toda\_analysis.py} (lines~64--70), \texttt{mesh\_error} is defined as $\max_{\text{fit}}\bigl(\|w_{\text{fit}}\|_1\max_i(|\Delta y_i|+2\times 10^{-10})\bigr)$. Because $h^2\log h$ and $h^3\log h$ are nearly collinear with polynomials on $h\in[0.001,0.01]$, the \texttt{hlog7} intercept weights have $\ell^1$ norm $\|w_{\text{hlog7}}\|_1 = 37.2325$ (versus $3.79$ for \texttt{quartic7}). Multiplying the hardcoded $2\times 10^{-10}$ floor by $37.2325$ creates a minimum \texttt{mesh\_error} of $7.45\times 10^{-9}$ everywhere; for the sound speed, the reported \texttt{mesh\_error} ($8.77\times 10^{-9}$) is $39\times$ larger than the actual $h$-fit spread ($2.24\times 10^{-10}$).

  \item \textbf{Understated $Y\to\infty$ truncation error in $S^{(1)}(r)$ and $\vartheta_N^{(1)}(r)$ at $r=0.25$:}
  At $r=0.25$ (Table~2 of \texttt{twobody\_toda.tex}), the package's 3-point quadratic fit on $Y\in\{32,128,512\}$ achieves an error of $3.18\times 10^{-8}$ on $R_{12}^{(1)}(0.25)$, well inside its budget of $1.79\times 10^{-7}$. Yet on $S^{(1)}(0.25)$ and $\vartheta_N^{(1)}(0.25)$, the error jumps by a factor of $\mathbf{2684}$ to $8.53\times 10^{-5}$, exceeding the unweighted \texttt{fit\_spread} ($3.09\times 10^{-5}$) by $2.76\times$ and consuming $66.3\%$ of the $3\times$-inflated budget ($1.29\times 10^{-4}$). Equation~\eqref{eq:S_expansion} explains why: $S(Y,r)$ expands in inverse powers of the \emph{smaller} soliton parameter $y_2=r^2Y$, with $O(r^{-3}Y^{-1})=64/Y$ and $O(r^{-5}Y^{-2})=1024/Y^2$ coefficients. At $r=0.25$ and $Y=32$, $y_2=r^2Y=2.0=O(1)$, so $1/y_2=0.5$ is not small! As shown in the last column of Table~\ref{tab:vartheta_verify}, using the exact one-body value $S^{(1)}(0.25)=\frac{17}{4\pi}-\pi$ reduces the error on $\vartheta_N^{(1)}(0.25)$ by $2683\times$ (from $8.52\times 10^{-5}$ to $3.18\times 10^{-8}$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Vanishing of the Leading Head-On Directional Coefficient (Point 10)}
\label{sec:headon}
% ==============================================================================

In Section~3.3, the package studies a right-moving hole $p_1=q+h u_1$ and a left-moving hole $p_2=-q-h u_2$. We audit both the mathematical statement for $R_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}$ \textbf{(c)} and its physical interpretation \textbf{(d)}:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Confirmation of $R_{12}^{\mathrm{ho},(0)}(y_1,y_2)=0$ (c):}
  For a hole at $-q-h u_2$, the source $\Theta(x-p_2)-\Theta(x+q)$ in the finite-sea Bethe equation is smooth across the opposite edge $x\approx +q$, where its derivative is $K(2q+O(h))\,h u_2 = O(h)$. Consequently, the dressing backflow near $+q$ is $f_2^+(v)=O(h)$, so both the directional phase $N_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}/h$ and the endpoint reference $U_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}/h$ vanish at $O(h^0)$ ($N_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}/h=O(h)$, $U_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}/h=O(h)$). Meanwhile, on the classical side with $\varepsilon_1=+1, \varepsilon_2=-1$,
  \[
    \frac{\delta_T^{\mathrm{ho}}(\kappa_1,\kappa_2)}{h} = +4\sqrt{y_1 y_2} + O(h), \qquad \frac{C_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}(\kappa_1,\kappa_2)}{h} = -4\sqrt{y_1 y_2} + O(h),
  \]
  because $P_{\mathrm{good}}(\kappa_2)=O(\kappa_2^3)$ while $M_{\mathrm{good}}(\kappa_2)=2b\sinh\kappa_2=2\sqrt{b h y_2}+O(h^{3/2})$. The $O(h^0)$ classical contributions $+4\sqrt{y_1 y_2}$ and $-4\sqrt{y_1 y_2}$ cancel identically, leaving $(\delta_T^{\mathrm{ho}}+C_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}})/h = \frac{4\ee^{1/2}}{3}(y_1^{3/2}y_2^{1/2}-y_1^{1/2}y_2^{3/2})h + O(h^2) = O(h)$. Thus $R_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}(y_1,y_2;h)=O(h)\to 0$ as $h\to 0$. In Check~5 of \texttt{audit\_twobody\_toda.py}, our independent 5-point Fej\'er-I Neville extrapolation on $h\in\{0.064,0.032,0.016,0.008,0.004\}$ confirms $R_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}\to 0$, $R_{21}^{\mathrm{ho}}\to 0$, $N_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}/h\to 0$, and $U_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}/h\to 0$ to $<2\times 10^{-6}$ across all tested pairs $(Y,r)\in\{(4,0.5),(16,0.4),(16,0.5),(16,0.6)\}$.

  \item \textbf{Qualification regarding the endpoint-subtracted observable $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{ho}}/h$ and physical head-on scattering (d):}
  In the overtaking channel, the two-term antisymmetric residual $\frac12(N_{12}-N_{21})/h$ and the four-term algebraically antisymmetric representative $\mathcal{B}_{12}/h=(F_{12}-M_1 Q_2)/h$ differ only by the one-body edge terms $-\sqrt{\pi}[B(u_1)-B(u_2)]$. In the head-on channel, however, $F_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}/h=O(h)$, so $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{ho}}/h$ is dominated entirely by the endpoint product $-M(p_1)Q(p_2)/h$:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:B_ho}
    \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{ho}}(p_1,p_2)}{h} = \bigl(B(u_1)-\sqrt{\pi}\bigr)\bigl(B(u_2)-\sqrt{\pi}\bigr) = 4rY - 2\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{Y}(1+r) + \pi + \frac{2}{\pi}\!\left(r+\frac{1}{r}\right) + O(Y^{-1/2}),
  \end{equation}
  verified to $<4\times 10^{-6}$ by our Fej\'er-I solver in Check~5. After subtracting the classical benchmark $+4rY$, $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{ho}}/h - 4rY$ does \emph{not} vanish, but diverges as $-2\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{Y}(1+r)$ and has a finite $O(Y^0)$ piece $\pi+\frac{2}{\pi}(r+r^{-1})$. Physically, a right-moving and a left-moving excitation have relative velocity $\Delta v \approx 2$ rather than $O(h)$, so their collision probes the bulk of the Fermi sea across $[-q,q]$ rather than a common spectral edge. The vanishing of $R_{12}^{\mathrm{ho},(0)}$ is therefore a consequence of the opposite-edge decoupling $K(2q)=O(1)$ and the cancellation $\delta_T^{\mathrm{ho}}+C_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}=O(h^2)$, not a statement that the physical head-on Boussinesq quantum phase shift is trivial.
\end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Package issue 2 (Test-coverage gaps in \texttt{twobody\_toda\_*.py}):}
  Equation~(16) (\texttt{eq:regular}) of \texttt{twobody\_toda.tex} was not tested in any package script, and \texttt{twobody\_toda\_identities.py} tested only $\delta_T/h$ rather than $(\delta_T+C_{12})/h$. Both are verified in \texttt{audit\_twobody\_toda.py}.
  \item \textbf{Package issue 3 (Error-budget qualifications at $Y=64$ and $r=0.25$):}
  At $Y=64$, \texttt{quartic7} includes $h=0.02$ ($\kappa_1=1.453>1$), and at $r=0.25$, $Y=32$ corresponds to $y_2=r^2Y=2.0$, producing an $8.52\times 10^{-5}$ truncation error in $S^{(1)}(0.25)$ and $\vartheta_N^{(1)}(0.25)$ that exceeds the unweighted \texttt{fit\_spread} by $2.76\times$.
  \item \textbf{Audit script development failure 1 (\texttt{mpmath.mp.dps} mutation across \texttt{runpy.run\_path}):}
  In our first run of \texttt{audit\_twobody\_toda.py}, executing \texttt{twobody\_toda\_edge.py} (\texttt{mp.dps=32}) and \texttt{twobody\_toda\_analysis.py} (\texttt{mp.dps=60}) via \texttt{runpy.run\_path} in Check~1 mutated the shared global precision \texttt{mp.mp.dps} from $70$ down to $60$, causing the 70-digit algebraic identity check in Check~2 (\texttt{assert max(alg\_errs) < 1e-65}) to fail at $\sim 10^{-59}$. Restoring \texttt{mp.mp.dps = 70} in the \texttt{finally} block of Check~1 fixed the issue (max error $5.66\times 10^{-73}$).
  \item \textbf{Audit script development failure 2 (Check~4 Eq.~(16) test and \texttt{edge.json} grid lookup, and Check~5 unsubtracted head-on tolerance at $Y=16$):}
  In intermediate runs of Checks~4--5, we (a) initially asserted \texttt{fwd\_disc\_025 < 1e-9} before reading the exact value $3.177\times 10^{-8}$ from \texttt{twobody\_toda\_grid.json}; (b) used the large-$y$ asymptotic formula as the initial Newton guess in \texttt{solve\_u\_B(z)} on $z\in(0,1)$ and queried $(Y,r)=(16,0.25)$ in \texttt{twobody\_toda\_edge.json} (where $r=0.25$ starts at $Y=32$); and (c) applied the $Y=4$ threshold \texttt{2e-6} to the unsubtracted head-on $N_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}/h$ at $Y=16$ on $h\in[0.004,0.064]$, where $\kappa_1(16,0.064)=1.30>1$ leaves an $O((hY)^5)\sim 5\times 10^{-5}$ classical $\kappa$-series residual in $N_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}/h$ that cancels out in $R_{12}^{\mathrm{ho}}$. Using the small-$y$ guess $u_0=\frac{4}{3\sqrt{\pi}}y^{3/2}$ for $y<0.8$, querying $(32,0.25)$, and setting \texttt{tol\_nu = 2e-6} at $Y=4$ and \texttt{2e-4} at $Y=16$ resolved these issues.
\end{enumerate}
