\begin{abstract}
\Lc We conduct an independent mathematical and numerical audit of
\texttt{phase2/\allowbreak onebody\_\allowbreak scheme.tex} and its three verification scripts
(\texttt{onebody\_\allowbreak scheme\_\allowbreak continuum.py},
\texttt{onebody\_\allowbreak scheme\_\allowbreak dictionary.py},
and \texttt{onebody\_\allowbreak scheme\_\allowbreak edge.py}). All three scripts execute cleanly in the
foreground and print \texttt{ALL CHECKS PASSED} without circularity.
Independent mode sums across four continuum regularisations (sharp wavenumber
cutoff, dimensional/analytic regularisation, heat-kernel damping, and the 2D
Euclidean Feynman bubble integral) and two lattice methods confirm the vacuum
sound-speed coefficients $s_{1,g} = -9/(4\pi)$ (good continuum),
$s_{1,b,\mathrm{ac}} = +9/(4\pi)$ (formally continued bad continuum), and
$s_{1,T} = +3/(4\pi)$ (quantum Toda lattice), refuting the assumption
$s_{1,\mathrm{cont}} = 0$ in \texttt{audit\_\allowbreak onebody.tex}.
Matching the low-energy vacuum elastic modulus fixes the local effective
Hamiltonian counterterm $\Delta\mathcal{H} = -\frac{3\hbar_B}{2\pi}u^2$
from homogeneous ($k=0$) vacuum mode sums alone, with zero input from soliton
data. Because the classical soliton canonical momentum satisfies
$P_0 = v\int u_0^2\,\dd x$, any counterterm $\Delta\mathcal{H} = \hbar_B\lambda u^2$
shifts both $s_1$ and the small-amplitude soliton slope
$C = \lim_{k\to 0}F(k)/|P_0(k)|$ by exactly $\lambda$, shifting $C_{\mathrm{ac}} = 2/\pi$
by $\Delta C = -3/(2\pi)$ to $C_{\mathrm{matched}} = 1/(2\pi) = C_T$ and leaving
the physical sound-subtracted soliton slope $C - s_1 = -1/(4\pi)$ strictly invariant.
We also confirm the diagnosis of the failed line~383 assertion in
\texttt{audit\_\allowbreak onebody.py} as a coarse-grid $O((hy)^3)$ quadratic extrapolation
truncation error ($2.2547\times 10^{-4}$ at $y=64$) on a true $h\to 0$ limiting
identity. \Ld The scheme explanation \textbf{(A)} stands for the leading
$O(\hbar_B^0)$ coefficient of the stipulated formally continued trace; it does
not construct a physical bad-equation fluctuation determinant, repair the real
stable-band (Scheme~A) resonance, or establish full finite-amplitude equivalence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Point 1: Independent recomputation of the vacuum sound-speed shifts}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Good continuum vacuum: $s_{1,g} = -9/(4\pi)$ across four regularisations}{Good continuum vacuum: s1g = -9/(4 pi) across four regularisations}}
\Lc (\scr) Expanding $\mathcal{H}_g$ around a homogeneous background strain
$\phi_0(x) = Ux$ ($u = U + \eta_x$, $\pi = \eta_t$, with $U < 1/6$) gives
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{H}_g(U+\eta_x) = \left(\tfrac{1}{2}U^2 - U^3\right)
  + (U - 3U^2)\eta_x
  + \tfrac{1}{2}\eta_t^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}(1-6U)\eta_x^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\eta_{xx}^2 - \eta_x^3,
\end{equation}
so small fluctuations have dispersion $\omega_g(q;U) = |q|\sqrt{q^2 + m^2(U)}$
with $m^2(U) = 1 - 6U$, and classical sound speed squared $c_{s,g,0}^2(U) = 1 - 6U$.
Because the cubic interaction $-\phi_x^3$ depends only on $\phi_x$ and not on
$\phi_t = \pi$, every external leg of the one-loop 1PI self-energy
$\Sigma_{\phi\phi}(p_t,p_x)$ carries a spatial momentum factor $ip_x$. Thus
$\Sigma_{\phi\phi}(p_t,p_x) = p_x^2\widetilde{\Sigma}(p_t,p_x)$ has
$\partial_{p_t}^2\Sigma_{\phi\phi}(0,0) = 0$: the canonical kinetic term
$\frac{1}{2}\pi^2$ receives no one-loop wave-function renormalisation, and the
thermodynamic sound speed squared at $U=0$ is $c_{s,g}^2 = 1 + \hbar_B W_g''(0) + O(\hbar_B^2)$,
giving $s_{1,g} = \frac{1}{2}W_g''(0)$ \Lc.

We compute $W_g(U)$ and $W_g''(0)$ by four independent regularisations (\scr):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Sharp wavenumber cutoff $|q|\le\Lambda$:}
  $W_{g,\Lambda}^{\mathrm{bare}}(U) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\Lambda q\sqrt{q^2+1-6U}\,\dd q
  = \frac{(\Lambda^2+1-6U)^{3/2} - (1-6U)^{3/2}}{6\pi}$.
  In the specified Wick normal ordering $:\!u^3\!: = u^3 - 3\langle u^2\rangle_{0,\Lambda}u$
  with $\langle u^2\rangle_{0,\Lambda} = \frac{\hbar_B}{2\pi}(\sqrt{\Lambda^2+1}-1)$,
  passing to $:\!\mathcal{H}_g\!:$ adds the counterterm density
  $+3\langle u^2\rangle_{0,\Lambda}U = -U\partial_U W_{g,\Lambda}^{\mathrm{bare}}(0)\hbar_B$.
  Subtracting the $U=0$ vacuum energy and this exact linear tadpole gives a UV-convergent
  $\Lambda\to\infty$ limit:
  \begin{equation}
    W_g(U) = \lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\Bigl[
      W_{g,\Lambda}^{\mathrm{bare}}(U) - W_{g,\Lambda}^{\mathrm{bare}}(0)
      - U\partial_U W_{g,\Lambda}^{\mathrm{bare}}(0)
    \Bigr]
    = -\frac{(1-6U)^{3/2} - 1 + 9U}{6\pi}.
    \label{eq:Wg}
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Dimensional / analytic (Riesz) regularisation:}
  Using $I_\varepsilon(M^2) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty q(q^2+M^2)^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\,\dd q
  = -\frac{(M^2)^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}}{4\pi(\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon)}$ (continued from
  $\operatorname{Re}\varepsilon > 3/2$) and subtracting $I_\varepsilon(1)$ and the corresponding
  normal-ordering tadpole $-6U I_\varepsilon'(1)$ cancels the regulator pole and yields~\eqref{eq:Wg}
  at $\varepsilon\to 0$ \Lc.
  \item \textbf{Heat-kernel damping and convergent second derivative:}
  Differentiating twice with respect to $U$ inside the mode integral gives a
  manifestly UV-convergent integral in $1+1$ dimensions:
  \begin{equation}
    W_g''(U) = -\frac{9}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty\frac{q\,\dd q}{(q^2+1-6U)^{3/2}}
    = -\frac{9}{2\pi\sqrt{1-6U}},
    \qquad
    s_{1,g} = \frac{1}{2}W_g''(0) = -\frac{9}{4\pi}.
    \label{eq:s1g}
  \end{equation}
  With exponential damping $e^{-\delta q^2}$, the integral is
  $-\frac{9}{2\pi}[1 - \sqrt{\pi\delta}e^\delta\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{\delta})] \to -\frac{9}{2\pi}$
  as $\delta\to 0^+$. Furthermore, rationalising the subtracted integrand on $q\in[0,\infty)$ as
  \begin{equation}
    q\left(\sqrt{q^2+1-6U} - \sqrt{q^2+1} + \frac{3U}{\sqrt{q^2+1}}\right)
    = -\frac{18U^2 q}{\sqrt{q^2+1}\bigl(\sqrt{q^2+1-6U}+\sqrt{q^2+1}\bigr)^2}
  \end{equation}
  eliminates catastrophic cancellation at large $q$ and reproduces~\eqref{eq:Wg} by direct
  infinite-line quadrature at $U \in \{0.02, -0.03, 0.05\}$ to $<10^{-65}$ (\scr).
  \item \textbf{2D Euclidean Feynman bubble diagram:}
  Evaluating the static one-loop bubble diagram with two cubic $-u^3$ vertices gives
  \begin{equation}
    W_g''(0) = -18\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\dd q}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\dd\omega_E}{2\pi}
    \frac{q^4}{\bigl(\omega_E^2 + q^2(q^2+1)\bigr)^2}
    = -\frac{9}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty\frac{q\,\dd q}{(q^2+1)^{3/2}} = -\frac{9}{2\pi}.
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\Lc This confirms \texttt{onebody\_\allowbreak scheme.tex} Eqs.~(18)--(20) and refutes
\texttt{audit\_\allowbreak onebody.tex}'s claim that $s_{1,\mathrm{cont}} = 0$ in the
bare-normalised (normal-ordered only) continuum theory.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Continued bad vacuum ($s_{1,b,\mathrm{ac}} = +9/(4\pi)$) and Toda vacuum ($s_{1,T} = +3/(4\pi)$)}{Continued bad vacuum (s1bac = +9/(4 pi)) and Toda vacuum (s1T = +3/(4 pi))}}
\Lc/\Ld (\scr) On the bad branch $\mathcal{H}_b$, the formal continuation
$F_{\mathrm{ac}}(k) = -iF_g(ik)$ used for the soliton trace corresponds in the
scaling family $\mathcal{L}_\sigma$ ($\sigma = c^2$ at $c=i$) to $x_g = -ix_b$
and $U_g = -U_b$, so $-i\int\dd x_g\,W_g(U_g) = -\int\dd x_b\,W_g(-U_b)$, giving
\begin{equation}
  W_{b,\mathrm{ac}}(U) = -W_g(-U) = \frac{(1+6U)^{3/2} - 1 - 9U}{6\pi},
  \qquad
  W_{b,\mathrm{ac}}''(0) = +\frac{9}{2\pi},
  \qquad
  s_{1,b,\mathrm{ac}} = +\frac{9}{4\pi}.
  \label{eq:Wbac}
\end{equation}
\Lc (\scr) We also verify~\eqref{eq:Wbac} directly from the bad dispersion
$\omega_b(q;U) = |q|\sqrt{1+6U-q^2}$ on its strain-dependent real stable band
$0 \le |q| \le \sqrt{1+6U}$: integrating
$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{\sqrt{1+6U}} q\sqrt{1+6U-q^2}\,\dd q = \frac{(1+6U)^{3/2}}{6\pi}$
and subtracting the $U=0$ constant $\frac{1}{6\pi}$ and the bad normal-ordering
tadpole $-3\langle u^2\rangle_0^{\mathrm{band}}U/\hbar_B = -\frac{3U}{2\pi}$ yields
exact agreement with~\eqref{eq:Wbac}. (Differentiating under the integral sign on a
fixed interval $[0,1]$ instead requires the Hadamard finite part
$\operatorname{FP}\int_0^1 q(1-q^2)^{-3/2}\,\dd q = -1$ because the band edge
$\sqrt{1+6U}$ moves with $U$.) As \texttt{onebody\_\allowbreak scheme.tex} rightly states,
the algebraic continuation~\eqref{eq:Wbac} is \Lc, whereas interpreting it as a
physical bad-equation vacuum despite the unstable modes $|q|>1$ is \Ld.

\Lc (\scr) For the Toda lattice at reference spacing $a$, $b = e^{-a/2}$, and
$\hbar_T = h = 3b\hbar_B$, a uniform Boussinesq strain $U = \partial_{x_B}\phi_B$
deforms the lattice spacing to $r = a - 6U$, multiplying the harmonic lattice
dispersion $\omega_T(K;r) = 2b e^{3U}|\sin(K/2)|$ by $e^{3U}$ across the fixed
Brillouin zone $K\in[-\pi,\pi]$. The one-loop zero-point energy per site is
$e_{1,T}(r) = \frac{h}{4\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} 2be^{3U}\sin(K/2)\,\dd K = \frac{2hb}{\pi}e^{3U}$.
Converting to Boussinesq energy density via $\Delta E_B = \Delta E_T/(6\sqrt{3}b^2)$
and $\dd x_B = 2\sqrt{3}\,\dd n$ gives $\hbar_B W_{T,\mathrm{bare}}^B(U) = \frac{e_{1,T}(a-6U)}{36b^2}
= \hbar_B\frac{e^{3U}}{6\pi}$. Subtracting the $U=0$ constant and linear pressure term yields
\begin{equation}
  W_T^B(U) = \frac{e^{3U} - 1 - 3U}{6\pi},
  \qquad
  (W_T^B)''(0) = \frac{3}{2\pi},
  \qquad
  s_{1,T} = \frac{1}{2}(W_T^B)''(0) = +\frac{3}{4\pi},
  \label{eq:WTB}
\end{equation}
which agrees with the non-perturbative Bethe scaling identity
$v_s(a,h) = \frac{a}{2}(q - h\partial_h q|_a) = ab + \frac{ah}{4\pi} + O(h^2\ln h)$ \Lc.

\paragraph{Why $s_{1,b,\mathrm{ac}} = 9/(4\pi)$ differs by a factor of $3$ from $s_{1,T} = 3/(4\pi)$ \Lc (\scr).}
In the Toda lattice, the classical potential in Boussinesq units is
$V_{\mathrm{Toda}}^B(U) = \frac{e^{6U}-1-6U}{36} = \frac{1}{2}U^2 + U^3 + \frac{3}{2}U^4 + O(U^5)$,
and a uniform strain $U$ rescales $\omega_T(K;U) = e^{3U}\omega_T(K;0)$ on a
\emph{strain-independent} Brillouin zone $K\in[-\pi,\pi]$, producing
$W_{T,\mathrm{bare}}^B(U) \propto e^{3U} = 1 + 3U + \frac{9}{2}U^2 + \cdots$.
In the truncated continuum bad Hamiltonian $\mathcal{H}_b$, the quartic vertex
$\frac{3}{2}U^4$ is omitted and $-q^4$ inside $\omega_b(q;U) = |q|\sqrt{1+6U-q^2}$ is
not multiplied by $e^{6U}$; rescaling $q = \sqrt{1+6U}\,\tilde{q}$ pulls $(1+6U)^{1/2}$
from the frequency and $(1+6U)$ from the measure $q\,\dd q$, producing
$W_{b,\mathrm{ac}}^{\mathrm{bare}}(U) \propto (1+6U)^{3/2} = 1 + 9U + \frac{27}{2}U^2 + \cdots$,
whose quadratic curvature is three times larger ($\frac{27}{2}$ versus $\frac{9}{2}$).

\section{\texorpdfstring{Points 2 and 3: Non-circularity of $\Delta\mathcal{H}$ and its exact effect on the soliton}{Points 2 and 3: Non-circularity of Delta H and its exact effect on the soliton}}

\paragraph{Point 2: Non-circularity of $\Delta\mathcal{H} = -\frac{3\hbar_B}{2\pi}u^2$ \Lc (\scr).}
Subtracting~\eqref{eq:Wbac} from~\eqref{eq:WTB} gives the homogeneous vacuum
energy-density mismatch between the Toda lattice and the continued continuum prescription:
\begin{equation}
  W_T^B(U) - W_{b,\mathrm{ac}}(U)
  = \frac{e^{3U} - (1+6U)^{3/2} + 6U}{6\pi}
  = -\frac{3}{2\pi}U^2 + \frac{3}{\pi}U^3 - \frac{9}{2\pi}U^4 + O(U^5).
  \label{eq:W-diff}
\end{equation}
Matching the vacuum elastic modulus (the $O(U^2)$ coefficient) in the same reference
coordinates uniquely fixes the local quadratic counterterm in the continuum effective
Hamiltonian:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\mathcal{H} = \hbar_B\lambda u^2,
  \qquad
  \lambda = s_{1,T} - s_{1,b,\mathrm{ac}} = \frac{3}{4\pi} - \frac{9}{4\pi} = -\frac{3}{2\pi}.
  \label{eq:counterterm}
\end{equation}
Audit of \texttt{onebody\_\allowbreak scheme.tex} and
\texttt{onebody\_\allowbreak scheme\_\allowbreak continuum.py}
(lines~18--57) confirms that $\lambda = -\frac{3}{2\pi}$ is derived \emph{exclusively}
from the homogeneous ($k=0$) vacuum mode sums $W_T^B(U)$ and $W_{b,\mathrm{ac}}(U)$:
neither the soliton profile $u_0(x)$, nor the continuum soliton coefficient
$C_{\mathrm{ac}} = 2/\pi$, nor the Toda Fermi-edge coefficient $C_T = 1/(2\pi)$
is used anywhere to choose $\lambda$. The linear $O(U)$ difference between
$W_{T,\mathrm{bare}}^B$ and $W_{b,\mathrm{ac}}^{\mathrm{bare}}$ is a background
lattice-pressure term $\propto M = \int u\,\dd x = 2k$, which is already fixed
to zero in the soliton dispersion by subtracting the Fermi-edge chemical potential
$E(q)$ on the Toda side and by the equal-mode-count endpoint plus Wick tadpole on
the continuum side \Lc.

\paragraph{Point 3: Why adding $\Delta\mathcal{H} = \hbar_B\lambda u^2$ shifts $C_{\mathrm{ac}}$ by exactly $\lambda = -\frac{3}{2\pi}$ \Lc (\scr).}
First, because $\Delta\mathcal{H} = \hbar_B\lambda\phi_x^2$ contains no time derivative
$\phi_t$, the canonical conjugate field $\pi = \partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\phi_t = \phi_t$
and the canonical field momentum functional $P[\phi,\pi] = -\int\pi\phi_x\,\dd x$ are
unchanged. Second, since the classical soliton $(\phi_0,\pi_0)$ is a stationary point
of $H_0 - vP$ ($\delta H_0 = v\,\delta P$), any $O(\hbar_B)$ deformation of the soliton
profile at fixed canonical momentum ($\delta P = 0$) contributes zero to the $O(\hbar_B)$
energy by first-order perturbation theory. Thus the shift in $F(k) = E_1|_P/\hbar_B$ is
simply $\Delta F(k) = \lambda\int_{-\infty}^\infty u_0(x)^2\,\dd x$.

Third, for \emph{any} travelling-wave soliton $\phi_0(x,t) = \Phi_0(x-vt)$, we have
$\pi_0 = -vu_0$, so the classical canonical momentum is identically
\begin{equation}
  P_0 = -\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\pi_0\phi_{0,x}\,\dd x = v\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} u_0(x)^2\,\dd x
  \quad\implies\quad
  \Delta F(k) = \lambda\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} u_0(x)^2\,\dd x = \lambda\,\frac{P_0(k)}{v(k)} = \frac{2\lambda}{3}k^3.
  \label{eq:exact-shift}
\end{equation}
Since $|v(k)| = \sqrt{1\pm k^2} \to 1$ as $k\to 0^+$, dividing~\eqref{eq:exact-shift}
by $|P_0(k)|$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \Delta C = \lim_{k\to 0^+}\frac{\Delta F(k)}{|P_0(k)|}
  = \lim_{k\to 0^+}\frac{\lambda}{|v(k)|} = \lambda = -\frac{3}{2\pi},
  \qquad
  C_{\mathrm{matched}} = C_{\mathrm{ac}} + \lambda = \frac{2}{\pi} - \frac{3}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{2\pi} = C_T.
\end{equation}
Higher-order local terms in~\eqref{eq:W-diff} such as $u^3$, and dispersive matching
terms such as $u_x^2$, have soliton integrals $\int u_0^3\,\dd x = \frac{4}{15}k^5$
and $\int u_{0,x}^2\,\dd x = \frac{2}{15}k^5$ of order $O(k^5) = O(|P_0|^{5/3})$, so
their contribution to $\lim_{k\to 0}F(k)/|P_0(k)|$ vanishes identically \Lc.

\section{Points 4 and 5: Naturalness of the renormalisation condition and the good equation}

\paragraph{Point 4: Naturalness and invariance of the sound-subtracted slope $C - s_1$ \Lc (\scr).}
In a gapless $1+1$-dimensional effective field theory, $u^2 = \phi_x^2$ is the marginal
elastic operator already present in the tree-level Hamiltonian $\frac{1}{2}\pi^2 + \frac{1}{2}u^2$.
Because adding $\Delta\mathcal{H} = \hbar_B\lambda u^2$ shifts the vacuum sound-speed
coefficient $s_1 \mapsto s_1 + \lambda$ and the small-amplitude soliton slope
$C \mapsto C + \lambda$ by the \emph{exact same constant} $\lambda$, the physical
soliton slope relative to the quantum sound cone,
\begin{equation}
  C_{\mathrm{rel}} := C - s_1 = \lim_{P\to 0}\frac{1}{\hbar_B}\left(\frac{E(P)}{|P|} - c_{\mathrm{sound}}\right),
\end{equation}
is an \emph{exact renormalisation-scheme invariant} (independent of $\lambda$) \Lc.
Without adding any counterterm, the continued continuum trace and the Toda lattice
already have the \emph{identical} sound-subtracted slope:
\begin{equation}
  C_{\mathrm{ac}} - s_{1,b,\mathrm{ac}} = \frac{2}{\pi} - \frac{9}{4\pi} = -\frac{1}{4\pi}
  = \frac{1}{2\pi} - \frac{3}{4\pi} = C_T - s_{1,T}.
  \label{eq:invariant}
\end{equation}
Depending on the coordinate/renormalisation convention chosen for the background sound speed,
there are three natural choices of $\lambda$ on the continued bad branch (\scr):
\begin{center}\footnotesize\setlength{\tabcolsep}{3.5pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
Renormalisation scheme & $\lambda$ & Vacuum $s_1$ & Soliton $C$ & Invariant $C - s_1$ \\
\midrule
1. Bare normal-ordered ($:\!u^3\!:$ only) & $0$ & $+9/(4\pi)$ & $+2/\pi$ & $-1/(4\pi)$ \\
2. On-shell continuum sound ($c_{s,\mathrm{ren}}=1$) & $-9/(4\pi)$ & $0$ & $-1/(4\pi)$ & $-1/(4\pi)$ \\
3. Toda-matched EFT (inherited Toda units) & $-3/(2\pi)$ & $+3/(4\pi)$ & $+1/(2\pi)$ & $-1/(4\pi)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Standard QFT/EFT matching between a lattice UV completion (analyzed in fixed bare
time coordinates where $c_{s,T} = 1 + \frac{3\hbar_B}{4\pi}$) and a continuum EFT
