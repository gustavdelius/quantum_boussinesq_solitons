\begin{abstract}
\Lc The leading rectangle is indeed
$-(15\sqrt2-20)/\pi=-0.386174647502482\ldots$.
Its construction, however, imports the Toda endpoint-product identity:
it tests a one-body coefficient inside a specified Toda frame, rather than
deriving that frame from the continuum determinant. The printed subleading
rectangle, profile sign, stability band, one classical series coefficient,
general tadpole expansions and numerical tables contain errors, despite all
three package scripts printing their success marker. Independent quadrature,
inverse Laplace transforms and unconstrained refits support the conditional
Toda numbers. The continuum canonical candidate differs from the Toda
directional antisymmetric coefficient by a nonconstant function of the ratio;
the equality of their rational terms does not repair this discrepancy.
\Ld V1 is not a completed validation. The physical comparison remains
conditional on a common quantum endpoint and momentum dictionary, and on
a defined bad-equation determinant. No scheme for the PI's good equation
is selected by this audit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conventions and classical checks (0--2)}
\paragraph{The positive profile is required (c; \scr, \texttt{symbolic}).}
Twice integrating the decaying travelling-wave equation gives
\[
 k^2u-u_{xx}-3u^2=0,\qquad v^2=1+k^2.
\]
The positive $u_+=k^2\operatorname{sech}^2(k\xi/2)/2$ solves it exactly.
The printed negative profile gives residual $-6u_+^2$, and cannot equal
$2(\log(1+e^{k\xi}))_{xx}$. The consistent potential has
$\phi=C+k\tanh(k\xi/2)$; its $\Pi=\int\phi_t=-2vk$ agrees with the printed
charge. The printed negative potential would instead reverse this charge.
Furthermore
\[
 vP_0-E_0=+\frac{2}{15}\sinh^5\beta,\qquad
 \frac{d(vP_0-E_0)}{dv}=P_0.
\]
The note prints the opposite sign, although its phase script checks the
positive sign. Squaring the potential tangent conceals the profile-sign
error in the kinetic norm.

\Lc Linearisation of the bad PDE yields $\omega^2=q^2(1-q^2)$.
The stable vacuum band is $0<|q|<1$, not $|q|>1$; the instability is at
short wavelengths. At infinity a localized soliton has this same laboratory
dispersion. Its velocity does not replace the vacuum band edge by
$\sqrt{1+\sinh^2\beta}$. Direct continuation of the Hirota coefficient at
$(\beta,q)=(0.7,1.3)$ has modulus different from one (approximately $1.839$),
so there is no real whole-line scattering phase of the asserted type.
Keeping $t=\sqrt{1+q^2}$ is not the bad real-frequency dispersion.
These errors contradict the controlling one-body scheme audit.

\Lc With $b=e^{-a/2}$ the Toda elastic constant is $b^2$, hence the physical
sound speed is $ab$, not the printed $a\sqrt b$.
Differentiating the stated lattice charges gives
$dE_T/dP_T=ab\sinh\kappa/\kappa\to ab$.
The action/Planck map $h=3b\hbar_B$ and $y=\beta^2/\hbar_B$ consistently
identify dimensionless phases at leading continuum order. They are not an
exact finite-amplitude equivalence: substituting $\kappa=\sqrt3\beta$ in
the lattice momentum yields
\[
 \frac{aP_T}{6\sqrt3b}-P_{0,B}=-\frac7{15}\beta^5+O(\beta^7).
\]
Thus ``identically'' must be restricted to the continuum action normalization.

\Lc No defect scattering or spectral calculation enters this package's
actual formulas. The errata dictionary remains $s=\sigma_{\rm vac}$ for
scattering and $\eta\sigma_{\rm vac}$ for the spectrum. The CZ field/action
dictionary is inherited as $u_{\rm CZ}=-3\phi$, $L_{\rm CZ}=9L$; it is not
used to choose a new convention. There is no defect-$s$ test here to certify.

\paragraph{Exact classical core confirmed (c; \texttt{continuum\_checks}).}
Using $P_-(K,W)=W^2-K^2-K^4$ directly, rather than the package's reduced
implementation, reproduces its factored coefficient and all four signed
action-gradient identities. Positivity follows explicitly by putting
$p=\beta_1+\beta_2$, $m=\beta_1-\beta_2$:
\begin{align*}
 A_{\rm ov}&=\frac{\sinh^2(m/2)}{\sinh^2(p/2)}
             \frac{1+2\cosh p}{1+2\cosh m},\\
 A_{\rm ho}&=\frac{\cosh^2(p/2)}{\cosh^2(m/2)}
             \frac{2\cosh m-1}{2\cosh p-1}.
\end{align*}
Both are positive off the equal-amplitude overtaking diagonal.
The oriented algebraic phase uses the package's label convention; calling
its extension to every ordering a physical collision phase needs that
orientation convention to be retained.

\Lc Formal division of the unfactored polynomial gives the correction
\begin{equation}
 \frac{A_{\rm ov}(\varepsilon,r\varepsilon)}{A_0(r)}
 =1+r\varepsilon^2+
 \left[\frac{r^2}{2}-\frac{13r(1+r^2)}{60}\right]\varepsilon^4
 +O(\varepsilon^6).
 \label{eq:Acorrect}
\end{equation}
At $r=1/2$ the coefficient is $-1/96$, rather than $+1/8$.
The script only checks the $\varepsilon^2$ term. The action expansions through
$\varepsilon^4$, head-on coefficient through $\varepsilon^4$, and canonical
$H,D$ expansions through $\varepsilon^2$ are independently confirmed;
the error in \eqref{eq:Acorrect} first affects the overtaking action at the
next order.

\section{Phonon integrals and one-body inputs (3--4)}
\paragraph{Verified function versus uncomputed determinant (c).}
The scale-free function is independently obtained from a direct phase
integral, not from evaluation of the dilogarithm against itself:
\begin{equation}
 I(a)=-\frac4\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}
        \arctan\!\left(\frac{a}{\tan t}\right)dt
 =\frac4\pi\int_0^a\frac{\log x}{1-x^2}\,dx.
 \label{eq:IK}
\end{equation}
Its dilogarithmic expression and $I(a)+I(1/a)=-\pi$ follow by differentiating
and fixing the $a\downarrow0$ constant; both are independently checked.
The symmetric endpoint offset is retained: $J=I+\pi/2$ in the $++$ chamber.
The old refuted claim of raw-phase antisymmetry is not used.

\Lc There is also an ordering issue. In the real good-contour integral
$I_{12}=\int\theta_1\theta'_2/(4\pi)$, the small positive-$q$ phases are
$\theta_j=4\arctan(k_j/q)$ and the negative-$q$ leading phases vanish.
Equation~\eqref{eq:IK} therefore has $a=k_1/k_2$, whereas this package sets
$r=\beta_2/\beta_1$. A direct quadrature of the full good phonon expression
at $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)=(10^{-5},5\cdot10^{-6})$ gives
$-2.000671508529$, approaching $I(2)=-2.000671508540$, not
$I(1/2)=-1.140921145050$. The continued small-root ratio also remains
$\sinh\beta_1/\sinh\beta_2$. A reversal or redefinition of the directional
integral must therefore be specified before identifying the package's $I(r)$
with that source. This test does not select a contour for the unstable bad
theory. The grid comparisons below grant the package its own $I(r)$ definition,
so this additional issue cannot manufacture the reported mismatch.

\Lc Inspection of all three complete scripts finds no computation of the
bad finite-amplitude $I_{12}$, no two-dimensional phonon quadrature, and no
nine-term dilogarithm test of the claimed
$c_{2,\rm det}^{\rm ov}$ or $c_{2,\rm det}^{\rm ho}=-8r/\pi$.
The phase script verifies $I(r)$ against a one-dimensional integral for
$\chi_2$ and the classical/frame expansions. Its output even records a
different, logarithmic head-on expression, without evaluating it.
The grid script inserts $-8r/\pi$ as data. Thus the asserted 60-digit
verification and the associated error budget are refuted; a particular
continued finite-amplitude determinant coefficient remains unestablished (d).
An $O(\hbar_B)$ counterterm does not change the leading quadratic operator
in loop counting (c), but this observation supplies neither its boundary
domain nor the missing integral.

\paragraph{One-body algebra survives conditionally (c; \texttt{symbolic},
\texttt{continuum\_checks}).}
Integrating $dF_{\rm ac}/dk=2\sqrt3 k\arctan(2k/\sqrt3)/\pi$ from $k=0$
and differentiating with respect to velocity independently reproduces
$F_{\rm matched}$, $C=1/(2\pi)$, $p_{\rm can,matched}$, and the displayed
canonical momentum Jacobian. In the bare momentum series the correct
$\beta^5$ coefficient is $-8\epsilon/(45\pi)$, not the printed
$-4\epsilon/(15\pi)$; the code uses the correct value.
These are properties of the continued trace, not a construction of a physical
bad-equation determinant. No Toda two-body number sets the energy's integration
constant: $F_{\rm ac}(0)=0$ and the vacuum-fixed counterterm are the inputs.

\Lc Direct quadrature of the actual stable-band transmission, retaining the
specified branch and its normal-ordering term, confirms
\[
 F_{\rm band}=F_{\rm ac}-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}(\sinh^2\beta+3/4).
\]
Thus its threshold constant is correct, but the package's explanation as
integration over $|q|>\sqrt{1+\sinh^2\beta}$ is false. The checks use the
real band $q=\sin t$, $|t|<\pi/2$. No new physical regularisation is selected.

\section{Frame choices, local terms and the projected measure (5--10,12)}
\paragraph{Three expressions, not three proved equivalent implementations (c)/(d).}
Direct charge differentiation verifies
$\gamma=(d\Pi/d\beta)/(dP_0/d\beta)$ and $p_{
\rm can}=-\epsilon F'/\sinh\beta$. Hence $F=2C\beta^3/3+O(\beta^5)$ gives
$p_{\rm can}=-2\epsilon C\beta+O(\beta^3)$ and
\[
 H_{\rm ov}\to0,\quad D_{\rm ov}\to-4C(r^{-1}-r),\qquad
 H_{\rm ho}\to0,\quad D_{\rm ho}\to4C(r^{-1}+r).
\]
The stated mode coefficients are checked by continuing the elementary
one-body root integral
$\sum_j c_j\sin\phi_j\operatorname{atanh}(\cos\phi_j)/(2\pi)$,
explicitly as a formal continuation, and reproduce its logarithmic slope and
constant. Substitution in the geometric shifts verifies its nonzero
$H_{\rm ov}$ and logarithmically divergent $D$.

\Lc Direct rescaling of the saddle gives $(S,P,E,v)\mapsto
(c_s^3S,c_s^4P,c_s^5E,c_sv)$ and
$5E_0-4vP_0=2\sinh^3\beta/3$.
Differentiating $c_s^3\delta_0(P_1/c_s^4,P_2/c_s^4)$ gives the printed
sound counterterm insertion, which vanishes as $\varepsilon^2$.
This is the \emph{change due to the counterterm}; it does not compute or erase
the pre-existing bare one-loop Darboux shift. Consequently the inference
``sound insertion tends to zero, therefore the full answer is phonon-only''
does not follow. A proposed Wronskian cancellation would be another input (d).
Likewise, $e-vp=F$ is invariant under $(e,p)\mapsto(e+v\delta p,p+\delta p)$;
it cannot choose the momentum entering a two-body phase.
\Ld Canonical momentum is natural for a completed collective action, but
that action's boundary-compatible reduction is not supplied here and is not
forced by the vacuum match.

\paragraph{Kinetic norm confirmed; projector inference qualified (c)/(d).}
For the printed tangent's square, direct finite-box integration and
$-\partial_q^2[\frac43\Gamma(2+iq/2)\Gamma(2-iq/2)]_{q=0}$ independently give
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}z^2\operatorname{sech}^4z\,dz=\frac{\pi^2-6}{9}.
\]
This verifies the positive finite part
$v^2(\pi^2-6)\dot v^2/(9k^3)$ alongside the linear divergence.
The claimed ``exact finite-$R$'' formula actually has an explicit exponential
remainder and is asymptotic. The finite pairing $dP_0/dv$ concerns a velocity
tangent, not a unit canonical-$P$ tangent; tangent normalization matters.
For coordinate tangents and the stipulated $\Omega=\int d\pi\wedge d\phi$,
direct integration gives $\Omega(\partial_X,\partial_v)=-dP_0/dv$;
reversing their order gives the positive sign. The package's
$\Omega(\xi_X,\xi_P)=+dP_0/dv$ therefore also requires explicit tangent
normalization and orientation, which its scalar derivative check does not test.

\Lc The existence of potential tails and the need to control the operator
domain are consistent with the earlier audits. The assertion that nonzero
canonical Poisson brackets imply $[\partial_t-v\partial_\xi,\mathcal P]\ne0$
is not a valid implication. A constant symplectic projector can commute with
a block-preserving evolution while its projected canonical pair has nonzero
bracket; the checker supplies this elementary matrix counterexample.
No explicit bad field projector or Schur determinant is constructed by the
package's script, which merely writes their descriptions into JSON.
\Ld The actual functional boundary problem remains open.

\Lc The package also overlooks a controlling result of
\file{audit_projected_measure.tex}. Independently recomputing
\[
 W=\begin{pmatrix}C&D\\-D&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_2),\quad C=\begin{pmatrix}0&c\\-c&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
gives $\det W=(d_1d_2)^2$ for every tail pairing $c$.
There is no tail-dependent finite-dimensional symplectic Jacobian available
to cancel $D_{12}$. An additional functional determinant is still possible
(d), but it cannot be inferred from that already-factorised Wronskian.

\paragraph{Counterterm statement: correct power counting, incorrect general series (c).}
Writing $t=c_{\rm tad}$, the actual series are
\begin{align}
 \frac{F}{|P_0|}
 &=\frac{3t}{\beta^2}+\frac2\pi+\lambda-\frac52t
 +\left(\frac{53t}{40}+\frac32c_5-\frac\lambda2-
 \frac{23}{15\pi}\right)\beta^2+O(\beta^4),\\
 \frac{p_{\rm can}}\epsilon
 &=-\frac{2t}{\beta}
 +\left(-\frac{2t}{3}-\frac4\pi-2\lambda\right)\beta
 +\left(\frac{2t}{45}-5c_5-\frac{4\lambda}{3}-\frac8{9\pi}\right)
 \beta^3+O(\beta^5).
 \label{eq:tadpole}
\end{align}
The printed equations omit the regular tadpole terms and partially specialize
$\lambda$ without stating the corresponding restriction in both equations.
The scripts check selected coefficients, setting $t=0$ before testing the
regular ratio. They never assert the displayed general formulas.

\Lc With $t=0$ and an analytic \emph{one-body restriction}
$\Delta F=c_5\sinh^5\beta+O(\beta^7)$,
$\Delta(F/|P_0|)=O(\beta^2)$ and
$\Delta p=-5\epsilon c_5\beta^3+O(\beta^5)$.
Since both geometric shifts are at most $O(\varepsilon^{-1})$ at fixed
$0<r<1$, their induced $H,D$ corrections are $O(\varepsilon^2)$.
This confirms exactly the requested invariance of $C$ and these $O(1)$
frame shifts. It does not prove absence of every independent two-body local
insertion, nor uniformity as $r\to0,1$, nor exclusion of nonanalytic or
nonlocal boundary terms. The tadpole cancels between separated charge sectors
by mass additivity; its vacuum coefficient is an inherited condition, not
independently fixed by this package.

\Lc Exchange antisymmetry of $H,D$ and the sound insertion follows by swapping
both amplitudes and orientation signs; it is also checked directly at finite
amplitude. The raw phonon integral has the nonzero symmetric endpoint offset.
All such algebraic antisymmetries retain the definition of the oriented phase.

\section{The rectangle: what agrees, what was imported (11)}
\paragraph{Independent four-corner expansion (c; \texttt{rectangle}).}
Let $f(y)=\sqrt y/u'(y)$. Conditional on the \emph{Toda} endpoint-product
reduction, the rectangle is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D_0(Y)=8[f(2Y)-f(Y)][f(Y/2)-f(Y/4)]
 -8[\sqrt{2Y}-\sqrt Y][\sqrt{Y/2}-\sqrt{Y/4}].
 \label{eq:rect}
\end{equation}
Additive one-body terms cancel. Independently expanding all four corners,
with $f(y)=\sqrt y+C/\sqrt y+B/y^{3/2}+\cdots$, gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D_0(Y)=-2C(15\sqrt2-20)
 +\frac{8C^2(\sqrt2-1)^2+(102-85\sqrt2)B}{Y}+\cdots.
 \label{eq:rectcorrect}
\end{equation}
The leading limit is confirmed, including the earlier frame audit's exact
number and the bare value four times as large. The printed subleading claim
is false: even $u'=1-C/y$ generates $B=C^2$ on inversion. The stated weaker
remainder $O(y^{-3/2})$ in $u'$ also permits an $O(Y^{-1/2})$ rectangle term,
larger than the asserted $1/Y$ correction. For the corrected Toda expansion
$u'=1-C/y-d_0/y^2+\cdots$, $d_0=1/48-C^2$, so $B=1/48$ and the $1/Y$
coefficient is $-0.344568583099\ldots$, not the package's positive
$8C^2(\sqrt2-1)^2$. Its script inserts $f=\sqrt y+C/\sqrt y$ and treats
that truncation as if it determined the next order.

\Lc The leading value differs from the quoted finite-$Y$ estimate
$-0.395751991872\pm0.021668942196$ at $Y=32$ by $0.009577344370$.
That arithmetic agreement is genuine; the uncertainty is an edge-fit spread,
not a confidence interval and not a bound on the finite-$Y$ bias.
The independent inverse-transform rectangle at finite $Y$ is tabulated below.

\paragraph{Circularity and identification (c)/(d).}
The energy coefficient was not fitted to a Toda two-body target. Its sole
lattice matching input is vacuum elasticity. However, the rectangle formula
itself, the endpoint identity and the finite-$Y$ Jacobians used in the
package comparison come from Toda; the code explicitly reads
\file{frame_analysis.json}. The package supplies no continuum two-soliton
determinant or endpoint operator calculation yielding \eqref{eq:rect}.
Consequently the result is a useful conditional one-body consistency check
inside a borrowed two-body frame, not an independently established common
continuum--Toda dictionary.

\Lc In Toda the inverse-rapidity Jacobian coefficient is
$A=2(s_1-C)$; it happens to equal $C=1/(2\pi)$ in the matched units.
A common sound-line shift changes $s_1$ and $C$ together and leaves $A$
unchanged. The continuum quantity $dP_{\rm phys}/dP_0$ is not, just from its
name, $du/dy$ for the Bethe rapidity. \Ld Equating them requires the missing
operator/coordinate map. Agreement of their first coefficients is evidence
for such a map, not its construction.

\section{Testing the rational-term lead and V1 (15--16)}
\paragraph{A shared function is not yet a shared observable (c).}
Grant the continuum package its ordered $r$ convention. Set
\[
 L_r=\frac2\pi\log r\log\frac{1-r}{1+r},\qquad
 D(r)=\frac2\pi(r-r^{-1}).
\]
Its canonical candidate and the conditional Toda result obey
\begin{align}
 C_{\rm ov}(r)&=J(r)+D(r)
 =\frac\pi2-\frac4\pi\chi_2(r)-L_r+D(r),\\
 \vartheta_N(r)&=\pi-\frac4\pi\chi_2(r)+D(r),\\
 C_{\rm ov}(r)-\vartheta_N(r)&=-\frac\pi2-L_r.
 \label{eq:gap}
\end{align}
Independent arctangent and regular-convolution integrals establish these
relations without feeding either target into a fit. In particular the rational
terms are identically equal, including sign, on the stated ordered chamber.
But $D$ is the continuum fixed-parameter to fixed-canonical-momentum
conversion; Toda's term arises when its \emph{directional} residual is
antisymmetrised with its endpoint/Jacobian product. They have not been shown
to be the same physical mechanism or frame transformation.

\Lc The proposed remainder $\pi-4\chi_2(r)/\pi$ is not the continuum raw
integral $I$ or antisymmetric integral $J$. It equals $I+H_{\rm mode}+\pi$,
or $J+H_{\rm mode}+\pi/2$, as functions on this chamber. Combining that
mode-only Hirota correction with $D_{\rm can}$ would splice two different
momentum prescriptions: the same $p_{\rm mode}$ also generates a divergent
Darboux term. A constant Maslov shift alone cannot remove $L_r$.
\Ld Thus the chi-function resemblance is a useful lead, but it does not
identify the remainder as the physical dressing contribution still to be
supplied by the continuum.

\Lc Reference terms are substantial. The Toda grid defines
$R_{12}=[\Phi(p_1,p_2)-\Phi(p_1,e_2)-\delta_T-C_{12}]/h$ and
$\vartheta_N=(R_{12}-R_{21})/2$. It does not define a Gamma-relative scalar
phase. Its endpoint-antisymmetric representative instead contains, relative
to the classical phase, $2\sqrt{\pi Y}(1-r)$ in overtaking and
$-2\sqrt{\pi Y}(1+r)+\pi+D_{\rm ho}$ in head-on, before lower corrections.
Those different references cannot be silently discarded. In the directional
head-on comparison Toda tends to zero, while the continuum canonical
candidate tends to $D_{\rm ho}=2(r+r^{-1})/\pi$.
