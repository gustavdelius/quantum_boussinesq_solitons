\begin{abstract}
We report an independent mathematical and numerical audit of the
\texttt{frame\_\allowbreak dictionary} package (\texttt{phase2/frame\_\allowbreak dictionary.tex},
\texttt{frame\_\allowbreak identities.py}, \texttt{frame\_\allowbreak edge.py},
\texttt{frame\_\allowbreak analysis.py}, and their JSON outputs), which addresses
ranked priority~\#1 of the Phase~2 master report: the common quantum
momentum and frame dictionary between the quantum Toda Fermi-edge limit and
continuum Boussinesq solitons at fixed physical observables.
\Lc\ The finite-sea Fredholm identities (dressed-energy endpoint identity,
thermodynamic pressure response, exact quantum momentum Jacobian, and four-term
endpoint reciprocity) are confirmed analytically and verified to 70~decimal
digits using an independent Fej\'er-I (Chebyshev-root) Nystr\"om solver. We also
derive a new exact finite-sea opposite-edge dispersion identity and supply direct
numerical $y$-derivative checks of both Jacobian equations in Eq.~(7) of
\texttt{frame\_\allowbreak dictionary.tex}, filling a test-coverage gap in the package.
\Lc\ An independent Fej\'er-I dispersion scan on $h\in\{0.06,0.03,0.015,0.0075\}$
with Neville and rational Pad\'e $[2/1]$ extrapolations confirms the central
entries of Tables~1--2, while qualifying their error budgets and asymptotic
identification: (i)~at $y=0.1$, the package's propagated mesh diagnostic for
$c_E(0.1)$ exceeds its edge-fit spread and is dominated by float64 cancellation
and root-finding tolerance amplified by $1/(hP_0)\simeq 1.04\times 10^7$;
(ii)~at $y=64$, the four-point fits include $h=0.04$ where $\kappa\simeq 2.05$
exits the small-amplitude regime; and (iii)~the sound-speed shift
$(v_s-b)/h$ equals the exact analytical constant $1/(4\pi)$, while $c_E(y)$
interpolates between $c_E(0^+)=1/(4\pi)$ and $c_E(\infty)=1/(6\pi)$ with
two-loop tail $-d_0/(2y)$.
\Lc\ The survival of the mixed classical-frame remainder $\mathcal D_h(Y)$ as
$h\to 0$ is confirmed across five alternative extrapolation bases and three new
point sets; we further prove that in any mixed rectangle the additive endpoint
subtraction terms cancel identically, reducing $\mathcal D_0(Y)$ analytically
to a pure function of the one-body Jacobian $u'(y)$ with exact asymptote
$\lim_{Y\to\infty}\mathcal D_0(Y)=-(15\sqrt2-20)/\pi\simeq -0.38617462$.
\Lc/\Ld\ Finally, the two-body antisymmetric counterexample $A_\lambda(y_1,y_2)$
in Eq.~(18) is confirmed as an algebraic ambiguity of the incomplete subset of
constraints tested by the package (one-body dispersion and symmetric
reciprocity), but is refuted as a physical ambiguity of the Toda/Boussinesq
problem: it is excluded by spatial cluster decomposition (which restricts
Darboux frame shifts to finite-rank bilinears of one-body charges, whereas
$A_\lambda$ has infinite Schmidt rank), by the microscopic Krein--Wiener--Hopf
differential equation, and by rapidity-ratio reflection parity and analyticity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Point 1: Re-derivation of the dressed-energy endpoint identity and quantum Jacobian}

\subsection{Analytic re-derivation from the finite-sea Bethe equations}
On $[-q,q]$, let $\Theta(d)=2h\operatorname{Im}\log\Gamma(1+id/h)+2d\log h$ and
$K(d)=\Theta'(d)=2\operatorname{Re}\psi(1+id/h)+2\log h$. Since $\Theta(-d)=-\Theta(d)$
and $K(-d)=K(d)$, the finite-sea integral operator
\[
 (\mathcal Af)(x)=tf(x)-\int_{-q}^q K(x-z)f(z)\,\dd z
\]
is real, symmetric, and parity-preserving on $L^2[-q,q]$. With $\mathcal A\rho=1$,
$\mathcal A\eta=x$, $\int_{-q}^q\rho(x)\,\dd x=1/a$, and
$\mathcal AF_p(x)=\Theta(p-x)$, self-adjointness immediately yields \Lc
\begin{align}
 P(p)&=p+\langle 1,F_p\rangle=p+\langle\mathcal A\rho,F_p\rangle
      =p+\int_{-q}^q\Theta(p-x)\rho(x)\,\dd x,\\
 E(p)&=\frac{p^2}{2}+\langle x,F_p\rangle=\frac{p^2}{2}+\langle\mathcal A\eta,F_p\rangle
      =\frac{p^2}{2}+\int_{-q}^q\Theta(p-x)\eta(x)\,\dd x.
\end{align}
Differentiating in $p$ gives $P'(p)=1+\int_{-q}^q K(p-x)\rho(x)\,\dd x=:D(p)$ and
$E'(p)=p+\int_{-q}^q K(p-x)\eta(x)\,\dd x=:V(p)$. Evaluating the sea equations at
$x=\pm q$ gives $D(q)=t\rho(q)$ and $V(q)=t\eta(q)$, while integrating
$P'(p)=t\rho(p)$ across $[-q,q]$ using oddness of $P$ gives $P(\pm q)=\pm\pi h/a$. \Lc

To re-derive the boxed endpoint identities in Eq.~(5) of
\texttt{frame\_\allowbreak dictionary.tex}, differentiate
$(\mathcal AF_p)(x)=\Theta(p-x)$ with respect to $x$ and use
$\partial_x K(x-z)=-\partial_z K(x-z)$ to integrate by parts in $z$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A_dxF}
 (\mathcal A\,\partial_x F_p)(x)+K(x-q)F_p(q)-K(x+q)F_p(-q)=-K(p-x).
\end{equation}
Taking the $L^2[-q,q]$ inner product of~\eqref{eq:A_dxF} with the even density
$\rho(x)$ and using $\langle\rho,\mathcal A\,\partial_x F_p\rangle
=\int_{-q}^q\partial_x F_p(x)\,\dd x=F_p(q)-F_p(-q)$ together with
$\int_{-q}^q\rho(x)K(x\mp q)\,\dd x=D(q)-1=t\rho(q)-1$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:end_D}
 t\rho(q)\big[F_p(q)-F_p(-q)\big]=1-D(p).
\end{equation}
Taking the inner product of~\eqref{eq:A_dxF} with the odd density $\eta(x)$ and
using
\[
 \langle\eta,\mathcal A\,\partial_x F_p\rangle=\int_{-q}^q x\,\partial_x F_p(x)\,\dd x
 =q\big[F_p(q)+F_p(-q)\big]-\big(P(p)-p\big)
\]
together with $\int_{-q}^q\eta(x)K(x\mp q)\,\dd x=\pm(V(q)-q)=\pm(t\eta(q)-q)$
cancels the bare $q[F_p(q)+F_p(-q)]$ boundary term and yields the dressed-energy
endpoint identity \Lc
\begin{equation}\label{eq:end_V}
 t\eta(q)\big[F_p(q)+F_p(-q)\big]=P(p)-V(p).
\end{equation}
Subtracting~\eqref{eq:end_D}--\eqref{eq:end_V} at $p=q$ (where $f_p^\pm:=F_p(\pm q)-F_q(\pm q)$
and $\overline P(p):=P(p)-P(q)$) confirms Eq.~(12) of
\texttt{frame\_\allowbreak dictionary.tex}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MQ_deriv}
 \mathfrak M_p:=ta\rho(q)(f_p^+-f_p^-)=-a\big[D(p)-D(q)\big],\qquad
 \mathfrak Q_p:=\frac{f_p^++f_p^-}{2a\rho(q)}=\frac{\overline P(p)-V(p)+V(q)}{2at\rho(q)\eta(q)}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{A new exact opposite-edge dispersion identity \Lc.}
Combining~\eqref{eq:end_D} multiplied by $v_s=\eta(q)/\rho(q)=V(q)/D(q)$ with~\eqref{eq:end_V}
eliminates the right-edge backflow $f_p^+$ altogether and yields an exact finite-sea
identity for the relative dispersion slope:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:opp_edge}
 \boxed{E'(p)-v_s P'(p)=\overline P(p)-2V(q)\big[F_p(-q)-F_q(-q)\big].}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:opp_edge} shows that any deviation of $E'(p)-v_s P'(p)$ from
$\overline P(p)$ comes exclusively from the opposite-edge backflow $f_p^-$
(\scr, Check~1).

\paragraph{Thermodynamic pressure response and exact quantum Jacobian \Lc.}
Differentiating $(\mathcal A_q f)(x)=g(x)$ with respect to $q$ at fixed $x\in(-q,q)$ and
fixed $h$ gives $(\mathcal A_q\partial_q f)(x)=\partial_q g(x)+K(x-q)f(q)+K(x+q)f(-q)$.
For $f=\rho$ ($\partial_q g=0$, $\rho(q)=\rho(-q)$), self-adjointness gives
$\partial_q n=2\rho(q)+\langle\rho,\mathcal A_q\partial_q\rho\rangle=2t\rho(q)^2$ and
$\partial_q P(p)|_{p,h}=t\rho(q)[F_p(q)+F_p(-q)]$. Since $a=1/n(q)$, the chain rule
$\partial_a=-a^{-2}(\partial_q n)^{-1}\partial_q$ confirms Eq.~(13) of
\texttt{frame\_\allowbreak dictionary.tex}:
$-a\partial_a P(p)|_{p,h}=[F_p(q)+F_p(-q)]/[2a\rho(q)]$.
Likewise, differentiating the momentum-inversion relation
$\overline P(q+hu(y))=P_0(\kappa)$ ($\kappa=\sqrt{yh/b}$) with respect to $y$ at
fixed $(h,a)$ using $\dd P_0/\dd y=(\dd P_0/\dd\kappa)(\dd\kappa/\dd y)=\frac{2h\sinh\kappa}{a}$
and $\dd\overline P/\dd y=hD(q+hu)\,\dd u/\dd y$ confirms Eq.~(7) of
\texttt{frame\_\allowbreak dictionary.tex}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jac_audit}
 \frac{\dd u}{\dd y}=\frac{2\sinh\kappa}{aD(q+hu)},\qquad
 \frac{\dd\overline E}{\dd y}=\frac{2h\sinh\kappa}{a}\frac{V(q+hu)}{D(q+hu)}.
\end{equation}

Inspecting \texttt{frame\_\allowbreak identities.py} and \texttt{frame\_\allowbreak edge.py}

\subsection{Three qualifications on the dispersion error budgets and closed forms}
While the separation of mesh and edge-extrapolation diagnostics in
\texttt{frame\_\allowbreak dictionary.tex} is transparent, our audit identifies
three substantive qualifications (\scr, Check~2):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Conditioning amplification vs.\ quadrature error at small $y$ \Lc:}
At $y=0.1$, the definition $c_{E,h}(y)=[\overline E(P_0)-E_0]/(hP_0)$ divides by
$hP_0(\kappa)\propto h^{5/2}y^{3/2}$, which equals $9.57\times 10^{-8}$ at $h=0.005$
(amplification factor $1/(hP_0)=1.045\times 10^7$). Consequently, IEEE~754 float64
subtraction roundoff and the root-finding tolerance (\texttt{xtol=6e-15} in
\texttt{frame\_\allowbreak edge.py:46}) are amplified into the reported mesh
difference $4.62\times 10^{-10}$. When multiplied by the $\ell^1$ extrapolation
weight factor $15.0$ of the four-point $h\log h$ fit on geometric $h$-grids, the
package's own \texttt{mesh\_propagated} diagnostic ($6.94\times 10^{-9}$) actually
\emph{exceeds} the reported \texttt{edge\_spread} ($5.86\times 10^{-9}$) at $y=0.1$.
\item \textbf{Exit from the small-amplitude regime at $y=64$ \Lc:}
The expansion parameter governing finite-$h$ lattice corrections at fixed $y$ is
$\kappa^2=yh/b$. At $y=64$, the four-point cubic and $h\log h$ fits include $h=0.04$,
where $\kappa=\sqrt{64\times 0.04/e^{-1/2}}\simeq 2.054$ ($\kappa^2\simeq 4.22>1$),
well outside the small-$\kappa$ regime.
\item \textbf{Exact analytical closed forms for $(v_s-b)/h$ and $c_E(y)$ asymptotics \Lc:}
Line~235 of \texttt{frame\_\allowbreak dictionary.tex} states that the extrapolated
sound-velocity shift $(v_s-b)/h\simeq 0.0795774724$ is ``a numerical estimate, not an
independently proved closed form.'' In fact, for $a=1$ it is the exact one-loop
Fermi-velocity shift
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vs_exact}
 \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{v_s(h)-b}{h}=\frac{1}{4\pi}=0.07957747154594767\ldots,
\end{equation}
which agrees with the package's cubic extrapolation ($0.079577471578$) to
$3.2\times 10^{-11}$ and with our independent Fej\'er-I Neville extrapolation
($0.079577471692$) to $1.46\times 10^{-10}$. Moreover, since
$\overline E(p)/P_0(\kappa)\to v_s$ as $y\to 0^+$ while integration of the bulk
dispersion relation $\dd\overline E/\dd u=v_s\dd\overline P/\dd u+\frac{\sqrt b}{3}h^{5/2}y^{3/2}+O(h^{7/2})$
against $\dd u=[1-\frac{1}{2\pi y}-\frac{d_0}{y^2}+O(y^{-3})]\,\dd y$ subtracts
$\frac{1}{12\pi}$ at $y\to\infty$, $c_E(y)$ interpolates monotonically between exact
analytical endpoints:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cE_limits}
 \lim_{y\to 0^+}c_E(y)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\simeq 0.07957747,\qquad
 c_E(y)=\frac{1}{6\pi}-\frac{d_0}{2y}+O(y^{-2})\quad (y\to\infty),
\end{equation}
where $\frac{1}{6\pi}\simeq 0.0530516477$ and
$-\frac{d_0}{2}=\frac{1}{8\pi^2}-\frac{1}{96}\simeq +0.00224848$. At $y=16$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cE_limits} gives $0.05319218$, matching Table~1 ($0.0531937$) to
$1.5\times 10^{-6}$. \Lc
\end{enumerate}

\section{Point 3: The mixed classical-frame remainder and its exact reduction}

\subsection{Exact reduction of the edge limit to the one-body Jacobian}
In Section~5 of \texttt{frame\_\allowbreak dictionary.tex}, the symmetric classical-frame
remainder at fixed scaled dressed momenta is
\[
 S_h(y,z)=\frac{\mathfrak M_y\mathfrak Q_z+\mathfrak M_z\mathfrak Q_y
 -C_{\rm cl}(y,z)-C_{\rm cl}(z,y)}{h},\qquad
 C_{\rm cl}(y,z)=-2\kappa_y\big[P_0(\kappa_z)-2b\sinh\kappa_z\big].
\]
Using the exact reciprocity identity $\mathfrak M_y\mathfrak Q_z+\mathfrak M_z\mathfrak Q_y
=t(f_y^+f_z^+-f_y^-f_z^-)$ and~\eqref{eq:end_D}, we have identically at every finite $h$:
\[
 f_y^+-f_y^-=1-\frac{D(q+hu(y))}{D(q)}.
\]
As $h\to 0$ at fixed $y>0$, the opposite-edge response scales as $f_y^-=O(h)\to 0$,
while $D(q)=\sqrt{\pi h/b}\,[1+O(h)]$ ($a=1$) and
$D(q+hu(y))=2\sqrt{yh/b}/u'(y)\,[1+O(yh)]$. Hence
$\lim_{h\to 0}f_y^+=1-\frac{2\sqrt y}{\sqrt\pi\,u'(y)}$, and since
$[C_{\rm cl}(y,z)+C_{\rm cl}(z,y)]/h\to 8\sqrt{yz}$, the exact $h\to 0$ limit of
$S_h(y,z)$ is \Lc (\scr, Check~3):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S0_exact}
 \boxed{S_0(y,z):=\lim_{h\to 0}S_h(y,z)
 =8\sqrt{yz}\left(\frac{1}{u'(y)\,u'(z)}-1\right)
  -4\sqrt\pi\left(\frac{\sqrt y}{u'(y)}+\frac{\sqrt z}{u'(z)}\right)+2\pi.}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:S0_exact} establishes two decisive facts:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Confirmation of \texttt{frame\_\allowbreak dictionary.tex} lines~339--346 \Lc:}
For a single pair $(y,z)$, the $-D(q)$ endpoint subtraction contributes the additive
one-body terms $-4\sqrt\pi\big(\frac{\sqrt y}{u'(y)}+\frac{\sqrt z}{u'(z)}\big)+2\pi$
in~\eqref{eq:S0_exact}. At $(y,z)=(4,1)$, keeping only the first term of~\eqref{eq:S0_exact}
gives $+3.7614$, whereas the full formula~\eqref{eq:S0_exact} gives $-13.1290$,
matching our extrapolated finite-sea value $-13.1290$ to $<10^{-6}$.
\item \textbf{Exact cancellation of endpoint terms in any mixed rectangle \Lc:}
In any rectangular second difference
$\mathcal D_0(y_1,y_0;z_1,z_0)=S_0(y_1,z_1)-S_0(y_0,z_1)-S_0(y_1,z_0)+S_0(y_0,z_0)$,
the additive one-body terms $-4\sqrt\pi\big(\frac{\sqrt y}{u'(y)}+\frac{\sqrt z}{u'(z)}\big)+2\pi$
cancel identically, and the remaining bilinear factorises:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D0_exact}
 \boxed{\mathcal D_0(y_1,y_0;z_1,z_0)
 =8\left(\frac{\sqrt{y_1}}{u'(y_1)}-\frac{\sqrt{y_0}}{u'(y_0)}\right)
   \left(\frac{\sqrt{z_1}}{u'(z_1)}-\frac{\sqrt{z_0}}{u'(z_0)}\right)
  -8\big(\sqrt{y_1}-\sqrt{y_0}\big)\big(\sqrt{z_1}-\sqrt{z_0}\big).}
\end{equation}
Thus the surviving mixed rectangle in Table~3 of \texttt{frame\_\allowbreak dictionary.tex}
is governed \emph{solely} by the one-body quantum Jacobian $u'(y)\ne 1$: the classical
frame subtraction $C_{\rm cl}(y,z)$ used the bare classical factor $\sqrt y$ rather than

\section{Point 4: Audit of the claimed antisymmetric two-body freedom}

In Section~6 of \texttt{frame\_\allowbreak dictionary.tex}, the authors exhibit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Alambda_audit}
 A_\lambda(y_1,y_2)=\lambda\frac{y_1y_2(y_1-y_2)}{(y_1+y_2)^3},
\end{equation}
and note that shifting $B(p_1,p_2)=\widehat\Phi(p_1,p_2)-\mathfrak M_{p_1}\mathfrak Q_{p_2}$
to $B+hA_\lambda$ preserves all one-body observables, axis vanishing
$A_\lambda(y_1,0)=0$, the tree-level action limit, and the symmetric reciprocity
sum $(B+hA_\lambda)(1,2)+(B+hA_\lambda)(2,1)=0$, while shifting $B/h$ at $O(h^0)$ by
$A_\lambda(Y,r^2Y)=\lambda r^2(1-r^2)/(1+r^2)^3$.

\paragraph{Is $A_\lambda$ a physical ambiguity or an artefact of the tested constraints?}
Check~4 of \scr\ confirms the algebraic properties of $A_\lambda$ \Lc, but proves that
it is \textbf{only an artefact of the incomplete set of constraints tested by the
package}, not a genuine ambiguity of the physical Toda/Boussinesq problem:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Spatial cluster decomposition and Darboux frame separability (excludes $A_\lambda$) \Lc:}
In the asymptotic two-soliton sector $|X_1-X_2|\to\infty$, local charge densities
cluster into single-soliton contributions, so the symplectic cross-term
$\sum_{i<j}\dd\Pi_i\wedge\dd M_j$ and the resulting Darboux coordinate transformation
$X_j\mapsto X_j'$ are generated by a finite bilinear $\sum_{a=1}^K f_a(y_1)g_a(y_2)$
of one-body state functions (Schmidt rank $\le 2$). Consequently, any frame correction
to the two-body phase must have vanishing $3\times 3$ minors
$\det_{1\le i,j\le 3}F(y_i,z_j)=0$. By contrast, $A_\lambda(y_1,y_2)$ has infinite
Schmidt rank: at $(y_1,y_2)\in\{1,2,3\}\times\{4,5,6\}$, Check~4 verifies symbolically
that $\det_{1\le i,j\le 3}A_1(y_i,z_j)=-\frac{18232351733}{8133427676989440}\ne 0$. Thus $hA_\lambda$
cannot arise from any redefinition of one-body soliton charges or Darboux coordinates.
\item \textbf{Microscopic Toda / Krein--Wiener--Hopf differential equation (excludes $A_\lambda$) \Lc:}
The package tested only the symmetric sum $\widehat\Phi(1,2)+\widehat\Phi(2,1)$,
whose kernel trivially contains every antisymmetric two-body function. However, the
Toda Fredholm equation fixes $\widehat\Phi(p_1,p_2)$ itself: in the Fermi-edge limit,
$(\partial_{u_1}+\partial_{u_2})\widehat\Phi_q(u_1,u_2)=2\psi_-(u_1)[\widetilde\Psi_-(u_2)-\widetilde\Psi_-(0)]$
is a rank-1 separable product of one-body functions, which uniquely determines
$\widehat\Phi_q(u_1,u_2)$ from the boundary condition $\widehat\Phi_q(u_1,0)=0$.
Applying $(\partial_{y_1}+\partial_{y_2})$ to $A_1(y_1,y_2)$ gives
$+(y_1-y_2)(y_1^2-4y_1y_2+y_2^2)/(y_1+y_2)^4$, whose $2\times 2$ separability
determinant at $(1,3)\times(2,5)$ is $\frac{277}{1920000}\ne 0$.
\item \textbf{Crossing / reflection parity and rapidity analyticity (excludes $A_\lambda$) \Lc:}
With $y_j=\beta_j^2/\hbar_B$ and $r=\beta_2/\beta_1$, $A_\lambda(Y,r^2Y)=\lambda r^2(1-r^2)/(1+r^2)^3$
is \emph{even} under $r\mapsto -r$ ($\beta_2\mapsto -\beta_2$), whereas the physical
one-loop residual $R_0(\infty,r)=-\frac{4}{\pi}(\chi_2(r)-r)$ is strictly \emph{odd}
in $r$. Furthermore, $A_\lambda(Y,r^2Y)$ introduces unphysical third-order poles at
$r=\pm i$ ($\beta_1^2+\beta_2^2=0$), where the Toda Bethe kernel is regular.
\item \textbf{What does \emph{not} remove $A_\lambda$ \Lc:}
The tree-level $O(\hbar^{-1})$ classical limit does not remove $hA_\lambda$ because
$hA_\lambda/h=O(\hbar^0)$ is one-loop order. Likewise, scalar three-body Yang--Baxter
and three-body Bethe momentum conservation $\sum_{j=1}^3 P(p_j)L=2\pi h\sum_{j=1}^3 I_j$
hold for any antisymmetric pair phase, though three-body linear sea backflow
$F_{p_2,p_3}=F_{p_2}+F_{p_3}$ combined with one-body charge separability excludes
non-separable frame terms.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Remaining physical open question \Ld.}
While $A_\lambda$ is an artefact of testing only symmetric reciprocity, a narrower
rank-2 question remains genuinely open \Ld: identifying the unique antisymmetric combination
of \emph{one-body} endpoint responses ($\mathfrak M_1\mathfrak Q_2-\mathfrak M_2\mathfrak Q_1$)
with the canonical collective-coordinate operators in the continuum Boussinesq Hilbert
space still requires the projected fluctuation determinant and zero-mode measure.
